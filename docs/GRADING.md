# Grading rules

This document says in plain words how a finished run is turned into PASS or FAIL. It is written for the project owner and for the DA50 instructor who reviews the rules before the trainer is shown to other pilots. The trainer is a training aid and does not replace the approved Airplane Flight Manual or an instructor.

The code is `src/engine/grading.ts` (the function `grade`), `src/engine/predicates.ts` (the condition checks) and `src/engine/sim.ts` (the fixed-step simulation that `grade` replays). The scenario files are TOON text files under `scenarios/`, validated against format 1 (`src/scenario/schema.ts`). The golden replays that pin these rules are in `tests/grading.test.ts`.

## What goes in and what comes out

`grade` receives three things: the scenario file as read by the parser, the aircraft (its controls, named quantities and physics), and the recording of the run. The recording is the list of control actions the pilot performed, in the order they happened, each with the time it happened, the control and the new position. A run on the Realistic level also records the onset (the second at which the failure began and the seed it came from). Nothing else is read: no clock, no random number, no screen state. The same inputs always give the same result, down to the last character of its JSON text.

The result holds the verdict (`pass` or `fail`), the reasons for a fail, one entry per graded step, the status of every branch of the checklist, the order violations and the harmful actions.

The level chosen before the briefing (Easy, Medium or Realistic) is not an input of `grade`: every level grades the same rules (R11). The level only changes what the briefing shows, how the run starts and what the debrief names. The one run datum that differs on Realistic is the recorded onset, which is part of the recording and not a level flag.

## The rules

1. **Starting point.** The run starts from the baseline: every control at its normal in-flight position, then the positions that the scenario file sets. On the levels Easy and Medium the quantities (for example oil temperature) start at the values the scenario sets, with the failure already present. On the level Realistic the controls are the same, but the quantities start at the base values of the aircraft (the cruise readings) and the failure begins at a seeded onset: until the onset the normal parameters act, from the onset on the faulted parameters of the scenario. The onset lies inside the window the scenario declares (`onset`, 1 to 120 s, on a 100 ms step boundary) and is recorded with the run, so a replay reproduces it. The starting point is not an action of the pilot and is not in the recording.
2. **Branches are decided at the start.** A checklist branch such as "If in green range:" applies when its condition holds at the starting point. A branch that does not hold is not applicable. A branch marked as decided during the run is open. A branch inside a branch that is not applicable is not applicable, and a branch inside an open branch is open.
3. **Only applicable steps are graded.** A step counts only when every branch around it applies. Steps in a branch that is not applicable or open are never graded in this version, and they are not missed. A step outside any branch is always graded. A forbidden action is watched only when it has no condition of its own, or when its condition holds at the starting point.
4. **Replay.** The recorded actions are applied one after the other in the order of the log. The time stamp is never used to sort them, so two actions with the same time keep their log order. During a run the quantities are simulated in fixed steps of 100 ms (`STEP_MS`): an action takes effect at the first step boundary at or after its time, the arithmetic is only addition, subtraction, multiplication and division, every quantity is limited to its range and rounded to its resolution after each step, and `grade` runs the same stepper as the live run, so the debrief and a replay of the recording agree. Steps and forbidden items still read the quantity values of the starting point and stay control-only; end states that depend on physics are designed in Phase 4 (see item 1 of the instructor review of phase 2). After every action, every graded step and every watched forbidden action is checked again.
5. **Steps and harmful actions.** A step becomes entered at the action after which its target holds, and the entry is cleared when a later action makes the target false again. A forbidden action is recorded every time it goes from not true to true after an action, with the time and the position in the log of that action, even if the pilot reverts it later. Several entries for one item are listed one after the other. The debrief lists a harmful action by its text; the time stays in the result for the replay but is not shown.
6. **Credit.** A step is credited when its target holds after the last action, that is at the moment the pilot declares the procedure complete. A switch that was moved to the right position and moved back is not credited. A target relative to the baseline, such as "power reduced by at least 10 percent of travel", is measured from the starting position: from 75 percent, 65 percent counts and 70 percent does not.
7. **Order.** In a group marked as ordered, take the credited steps in the order in which the file declares them. For every pair where the earlier declared step was last entered after the later declared step, one order violation is recorded. A single swap is therefore one violation. Steps that were not credited produce no violation, because they are already missed. A group that is not ordered never produces one.
8. **Verdict.** The run is PASS when every graded critical step is credited, when no harmful action belongs to an item marked as failing the run, and when no order violation belongs to a group marked as order-critical. Otherwise it is FAIL. The reasons are listed in a fixed order: the number of missed critical steps, then one entry for each order-critical group with a violation, then one entry for each item marked as failing the run that was triggered. Standard and optional steps, harmful actions of items that do not fail the run, and order violations of groups that are not order-critical are reported but do not change the verdict.
9. **Determinism.** Every list in the result follows the declared order of the scenario file, depth first, never the order of keys of an object. A recording for another scenario, or one that names a control the aircraft does not have, is a programming error and is refused with an exception, not graded.

10. **Outcome (R10).** A scenario can name outcomes: a condition on the quantities that, once it has held for its hold time (0 to 600 s), ends the run by itself. An outcome ends the run as FAIL, whatever the pilot did, and it is the first reason in the list (before the missed steps, the order and the harmful actions). Events recorded after the outcome time are not graded. The hold timer counts only while the condition holds and starts again from zero when it is interrupted. If two outcomes would fire in the same step, the one declared first wins. A run without a declared outcome is graded as before.
11. **Reaction time and level (R11).** On the level Realistic the debrief shows how long after the onset the first graded action came ("First action N s after the onset.") or that there was none. The time is display only: there is no time limit, nothing in the verdict depends on it, and the level is never an input of `grade`.
12. **Before the onset (R12).** On a recording with an onset, a step whose target already holds at the onset earns no credit from what was done before it: it is credited only after the pilot has made it false and true again at or after the onset, so a run that does nothing after the onset fails like on the other levels. The debrief adds "Steps done before the onset do not count." to the missed-steps line. Harmful actions are still recorded at any time. Recordings without an onset (Easy, Medium) are unaffected.

Further notes:

- A step that is already true at the starting point would be credited by a run that does nothing. The file check of the validation command rejects such a step (lint rule L5). It also rejects a file in which no critical step is graded at the start (lint rule L11). So a run that does nothing always fails in a shipped scenario.
- A tap that was taken back with the double tap gesture is removed from the log before grading, so retracted actions do not exist for the grader.

## Golden recordings

The file `tests/fixtures/recordings.json` holds 23 recordings, grouped here by scenario. The scenario `eng-oil-temp-green` is the blueprint; `ordered-critical`, `elec-ordered` and `fire-ordered` are synthetic copies in which the groups are ordered and order-critical (`tests/fixtures/scenarios/`), because the real procedures are unordered by default.

| ------------------ | ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| golden             | eng-oil-temp-green | 8000 power to 60; 15000 cowl flap open                                            | PASS, no reasons, no harmful action                                                                              |
| do-nothing         | eng-oil-temp-green | nothing                                                                           | FAIL, two critical steps missed                                                                                  |

Escalation and recovery of ENG OIL TEMP (completed at 600 000 ms): `do-nothing-long` ends with the outcome `engine_failure` as the first reason (the run fires at 42.6 s of run time, so nothing after it is graded) followed by the two missed critical steps; `golden-long` (the golden actions, then waiting) never escalates and passes.

Electrical Failure (ALTN 1 FAIL, scenario `elec-altn1-fail`, times in ms): `elec-golden` (6000 ALTERNATOR 1 off; 9000 ESS BUS on) PASS; `elec-golden-long` the same, 600 000 ms, PASS, never escalates; `elec-do-nothing` FAIL with the missed critical steps; `elec-do-nothing-long` ends with the outcome `main_bus_lost` (226.3 s of run time, the battery runs down); `elec-forbidden` (5000 ALTERNATOR 2 off; 8000 ALTERNATOR 1 off; 11000 ESS BUS on) FAIL only because switching ALTERNATOR 2 off fails the run, both critical steps are credited; `elec-wrong-order` (ESS BUS on, then ALTERNATOR 1 off) PASS on the real file, and `elec-wrong-order-ordered` on the synthetic ordered copy FAIL with one order violation.

Engine Fire (scenario `eng-fire-in-flight`, times in ms): `fire-golden` (2000 squawk 7700; 4000 fresh air; 5500 ECS SHUTOFF pulled; 7000 ENGINE MASTER off; 8500 FUEL selector off; 10000 ELECT. MASTER off) PASS with the optional squawk step done; `fire-golden-long` the same over 600 000 ms, PASS, never escalates; `fire-no-squawk` the same without the squawk, still PASS because that step is optional; `fire-do-nothing` FAIL with the five critical steps missed; `fire-do-nothing-long` ends with the outcome `fire_spreads`; `fire-forbidden` (the golden actions plus the EMERG FUEL PUMP switched on at 3000) FAIL because the pump fails the run; `fire-wrong-order` (the shutdown steps in reverse) PASS on the real file and `fire-wrong-order-ordered` on the synthetic copy FAIL with three order violations and one order reason.

Further cases in the tests: power at 65 percent credits the power step and 70 percent does not; power reduced and then raised again to the baseline is not credited; the same scenario with the item not marked as failing the run grades the `forbidden` recording PASS and only reports the action; an order violation in a group that is not order-critical is listed but keeps the PASS; steps in a branch that does not apply are not graded; a forbidden item whose condition does not hold at the start is never reported.

## Instructor review (phase 2 UAT, 2026-10-22)

The DA50 instructor reviewed the first gradable scenario. The answers to the four open questions and to the question of times are recorded here.

1. **Holds at completion.** The end state at completion is kept for now: a step is credited only if its target is still true when the pilot declares the procedure complete. Which end states are acceptable depends on the scenario (an action that helped but leaves a different end state can be fine). Scenario-specific acceptable end states are designed with the physics in Phase 4.
2. **Harmful actions.** They are defined per scenario. In ENG OIL TEMP, increasing power fails the run (`fails_run: true` on the item "power increased").
3. **Order.** The default stays "order does not matter". A group can be marked ordered and order-critical where the real procedure needs it, for example a later emergency gear extension (pull the handle, then the gear lever down). Nothing changes in the engine.
4. **Branches decided during the run.** They come in a later phase, as planned.
5. **Times.** There is no timing pressure in ENG OIL TEMP, and the debrief shows no time on a harmful action. Optional time limits per step ("reduce power within 20 s") stay possible for time-critical scenarios; the limit and its reference point (run start or onset) belong with Phase 3.
6. **Pass with recommendation.** A third verdict was proposed (PASS when everything critical is fine but something was not perfect). The owner said it may come later; name and wording are not decided. It is recorded, not built: see [the pending todo](../.planning/todos/pending/2026-10-22-pass-with-recommendation.md).

## Instructor review (phase 3, 2026-10-23)

Phase 3 made the quantities move and added two scenarios. Every value below was chosen by default while the phase was built without the instructor; none is taken from a measurement, and the tests pin the behaviour (the directions and relations), not the realism of the numbers. Each item says what was chosen. A different value is a data edit in the file named; the grading rules above do not change. The same items are listed for the owner in [PANEL-REVIEW.md](PANEL-REVIEW.md), section "Phase 3: owner review".

1. **Physics constants (A1).** The models are first-order lags toward a target, written as data in `src/aircraft/da50rg/physics.ts`. Time constants: oil temperature 60 s, coolant 40 s, gearbox 60 s, fuel temperature 60 s, engine speed 2 s, load 1 s, oil pressure 2 s, fuel pressure 1 s, alternator currents 1.5 s, bus voltage 1 s, fire intensity 30 s. The cruise base values are the equilibrium of every model (oil temperature 91 degrees C, speed 2100 rpm, load 70 percent, oil pressure 5 bar, coolant 85, gearbox 75, fuel temperature 20, fuel pressure 0.4 bar, 20 gallons per tank, 28 V, alternator 1 at 30 A and alternator 2 at 10 A, battery 100 percent). The time scale is real, not compressed: the oil temperature of an ignored ENG OIL TEMP run needs about 27 s to cross 140 degrees. The fuel burn is 0.1 gallon per minute at cruise (left tank only), the transfer 1 gallon per minute. The degraded oil cooling of ENG OIL TEMP is the scenario parameter 0.55 (normal 1); an open cowl flap helps by a factor of 1.3 and a power reduction lowers the heat.
2. **ALTN 1 FAIL stays on after ALTERNATOR 1 OFF (A2).** The caution is derived from the current of alternator 1 (at most 2.5 A), not from the switch. With the failure present the current is zero from the start, so switching ALTERNATOR 1 off, as the procedure asks, does not put the caution out. Whether the real caution goes out then is open.
3. **Bus model (A3).** Alternator 1 delivers while the engine runs with ALTERNATOR 1 on, its breaker in, ELECT. MASTER on and the scenario parameter above 0. Alternator 2 runs with its switch on, its breaker in and ELECT. MASTER on, and feeds the main bus only with ESS BUS on (35 A). Without any supply the battery drains by 25 percent per minute (empty in about 4 minutes) and charges by 10 percent per minute with one; on the battery alone the bus reads 22 plus 4 times the battery fraction in volts; ELECT. MASTER off reads 20 V. Only breakers `cb.alt1` and `cb.alt2` take their alternator off; every other breaker effect is open.
4. **Outcomes, thresholds and hold times (A4).** ENG OIL TEMP: `engine_failure`, oil temperature at or above 140 degrees C for 15 s ("Engine failure from overheating"). Electrical Failure: `main_bus_lost`, bus voltage at or below 22.5 V for 10 s ("Total loss of main bus power"). Engine Fire: `fire_spreads`, fire intensity at or above 85 percent for 20 s ("Fire spreads beyond the engine compartment"). The CAS rules: ENG OIL TEMP warning, OIL PRES warning on both red oil pressure bands (placed after ENG OIL TEMP), ALTN 1 FAIL caution.
5. **Onset windows (A8).** The failure begins at a random second inside 5 to 20 s in all three scenarios on the level Realistic. The seed is drawn by the application when the run starts and recorded.
6. **Forbidden lists (A8).** ENG OIL TEMP: increasing power fails the run. Electrical Failure: ALTERNATOR 2 off and ELECT. MASTER off each fail the run. Engine Fire: switching the EMERG FUEL PUMP on fails the run (it feeds the fire); increasing power is reported only.
7. **Order of the new procedures.** The groups of the Electrical Failure and Engine Fire scenarios are unordered, the planner default (RESEARCH Open Question 4); only the synthetic `elec-ordered` and `fire-ordered` copies are ordered. The instructor decides whether, for example, the engine shutdown (ENGINE MASTER, FUEL selector, ELECT. MASTER) must be in sequence.
8. **The optional squawk step.** In Engine Fire squawk 7700 on the transponder is an optional step and an own addition: it is not a line of the manual. It is credited only for that code and never changes the verdict.
9. **Engine Fire steps on the FUEL selector and the ECS SHUTOFF.** The scenario grades the FUEL selector at OFF and the ECS SHUTOFF pulled as critical steps, next to fresh air, ENGINE MASTER and ELECT. MASTER. Both controls were supplied by the owner on 2026-10-22 (descriptions only, no photo) and built in plan 03-10; the 225 degree OFF angle of the selector is unverified, see [PANEL-REVIEW.md](PANEL-REVIEW.md). The fire model: the selector OFF cuts the fuel like ENGINE MASTER off, the fire is fed 0.9 with the engine running, 1 with the EMERG FUEL PUMP on as well, 0.6 with the pump alone and 0 with the fuel cut, and it heats the oil by at most 30, the coolant by 30 and the gearbox by 40 degrees.
10. **The 120 s MAX run.** Pressing MAX with the de-ice mode on HIGH lights the top lamp for 120 s of run time; it ends early when HIGH is left. The 120 s is from the owner's finding, not a measurement. It works only during a run.
