# Grading rules

This document says in plain words how a finished run is turned into PASS or FAIL. It is written for the project owner and for the DA50 instructor who reviews the rules before the trainer is shown to other pilots. The trainer is a training aid and does not replace the approved Airplane Flight Manual or an instructor.

The code is `src/engine/grading.ts` (the function `grade`), `src/engine/predicates.ts` (the condition checks) and `src/engine/sim.ts` (the fixed-step simulation that `grade` replays). The scenario files are TOON text files under `scenarios/`, validated against format 1 (`src/scenario/schema.ts`). The golden replays that pin these rules are in `tests/grading.test.ts`.

## What goes in and what comes out

`grade` receives three things: the scenario file as read by the parser, the aircraft (its controls, named quantities and physics), and the recording of the run. The recording is the list of control actions the pilot performed, in the order they happened, each with the time it happened, the control and the new position. A run on the Realistic level also records the onset (the second at which the failure began and the seed it came from). A scenario with variants also records the variant the run drew (its id and the seed, R13). Since Phase 7 the recording also holds the decisions: every press of a further-action button of the run, each with the time it was made, the option id and an optional on flag, in the order made (R16). Nothing else is read: no clock, no random number, no screen state. The same inputs always give the same result, down to the last character of its JSON text.

The result holds the verdict (`pass` or `fail`), the reasons for a fail, one entry per graded step, the status of every branch of the checklist, the order violations and the harmful actions. A scenario that declares further actions, or a recording that holds decisions, adds one more entry, `further` (R16 to R18); without either the key is absent and the result is the same text as before Phase 7.

The level chosen before the briefing (Easy, Medium or Realistic) is not an input of `grade`: every level grades the same rules (R11). The level only changes what the briefing shows, how the run starts and what the debrief names. The one run datum that differs on Realistic is the recorded onset, which is part of the recording and not a level flag. No mode exists (Phase 7, plan 07-04). Every run has a checklist sheet; its marks are the grade of the partial recording (`result.steps[].credited`, rule R12 applies) read while the sheet is shown, they are not stored, counted or penalised and change nothing. On Realistic the pilot can choose any available checklist to look at and the last choice is named in the debrief; neither the choice nor the HINT box (the situation name and text of the file) is an input of `grade` or part of the recording. The same recording gives the same result at every level, with the sheet shown or hidden and with any checklist choice. Since plan 07-06 the sheet has more display state beside the recording, and none of it is an input of `grade`: the ticks the user puts on rows of the sheet (a row the program does not check can be ticked green and cleared again, cleared on start, Retry, Next situation, leave and when the debrief closes), the checklist the pilot looked at on Realistic (also a foreign one: it is marked green from what the pilot really did on the panel, by grading the same events against that file, which never produces a verdict), the tab of the chooser, the position of a scenario in the checklist catalog (it only decides the order and the group of the lists) and the Avoid picks of R21 (they are recorded decisions but unrated). The JSON of the recording and of the grade is the same with and without ticks and with and without a foreign checklist on the sheet (a unit case compares them).

A retry is a new run (Phase 7): nothing of the graded run carries over into it, no event, no decision, no checklist choice, no clock time. Only the two records that make the situation the same are reused, the onset and the variant (see "Retry and next situation (phase 7)"), and the recording of the retry is made from its own run like any other. The recording description above stays current: events, onset, variant and decisions, and nothing else.

## The rules

1. **Starting point.** The run starts from the baseline: every control at its normal in-flight position, then the positions that the scenario file sets. On the levels Easy and Medium the quantities (for example oil temperature) start at the values the scenario sets, with the failure already present. On the level Realistic the controls are the same, but the quantities start at the base values of the aircraft (the cruise readings) laid over by the quantities of the scenario's `onset` block (for ENG OIL TEMP the oil temperature just below the yellow band), and the failure begins at a seeded onset: until the onset the normal parameters act, from the onset on the faulted parameters of the scenario, laid over by the parameters of its `onset` block. Easy and Medium never read the `onset` block. The onset lies inside the window the scenario declares (`onset`, 1 to 120 s, on a 100 ms step boundary) and is recorded with the run, so a replay reproduces it. The starting point is not an action of the pilot and is not in the recording.
2. **Branches are decided at the start.** A checklist branch such as "If in green range:" applies when its condition holds at the starting point. A branch that does not hold is not applicable. A branch marked as decided during the run is open. A branch inside a branch that is not applicable is not applicable, and a branch inside an open branch is open.
3. **Only applicable steps are graded.** A step counts only when every branch around it applies. Steps in a branch that is not applicable or open are never graded in this version, and they are not missed. A step outside any branch is always graded. A forbidden action is watched only when it has no condition of its own, or when its condition holds at the starting point.
4. **Replay.** The recorded actions are applied one after the other in the order of the log. The time stamp is never used to sort them, so two actions with the same time keep their log order. During a run the quantities are simulated in fixed steps of 100 ms (`STEP_MS`): an action takes effect at the first step boundary at or after its time, the arithmetic is only addition, subtraction, multiplication and division, every quantity is limited to its range and rounded to its resolution after each step, and `grade` runs the same stepper as the live run, so the debrief and a replay of the recording agree. Steps and forbidden items still read the quantity values of the starting point and stay control-only; end states that depend on physics are designed in Phase 4 (see item 1 of the instructor review of phase 2). After every action, every graded step and every watched forbidden action is checked again.
5. **Steps and harmful actions.** A step becomes entered at the action after which its target holds, and the entry is cleared when a later action makes the target false again. A forbidden action is recorded every time it goes from not true to true after an action, with the time and the position in the log of that action, even if the pilot reverts it later. Several entries for one item are listed one after the other. The debrief lists a harmful action by its text; the time stays in the result for the replay but is not shown.
6. **Credit.** A step is credited when its target holds after the last action, that is at the moment the pilot declares the procedure complete. A switch that was moved to the right position and moved back is not credited. A target relative to the baseline, such as "power reduced by at least 10 percent of travel", is measured from the starting position: from 75 percent, 65 percent counts and 70 percent does not.
7. **Order.** In a group marked as ordered, take the credited steps in the order in which the file declares them. For every pair where the earlier declared step was last entered after the later declared step, one order violation is recorded. A single swap is therefore one violation. Steps that were not credited produce no violation, because they are already missed. A group that is not ordered never produces one.
8. **Verdict.** The run is PASS when every graded critical step is credited, when no harmful action belongs to an item marked as failing the run, and when no order violation belongs to a group marked as order-critical. Otherwise it is FAIL. The reasons are listed in a fixed order: the number of missed critical steps, then one entry for each order-critical group with a violation, then one entry for each item marked as failing the run that was triggered. Standard and optional steps, harmful actions of items that do not fail the run, and order violations of groups that are not order-critical are reported but do not change the verdict.
9. **Determinism.** Every list in the result follows the declared order of the scenario file, depth first, never the order of keys of an object. A recording for another scenario, or one that names a control the aircraft does not have, is a programming error and is refused with an exception, not graded.

10. **Outcome (R10).** A scenario can name outcomes: a condition on the quantities that, once it has held for its hold time (0 to 600 s), ends the run by itself. An outcome ends the run as FAIL, whatever the pilot did, and it is the first reason in the list (before the missed steps, the order and the harmful actions). Events recorded after the outcome time are not graded. The hold timer counts only while the condition holds and starts again from zero when it is interrupted. If two outcomes would fire in the same step, the one declared first wins. A run without a declared outcome is graded as before.
11. **Reaction time and level (R11).** On the level Realistic the debrief shows how long after the onset the first graded action came ("First action N s after the onset.") or that there was none. The time is display only: there is no time limit, nothing in the verdict depends on it, and the level is never an input of `grade`.
12. **Before the onset (R12).** On a recording with an onset, a step whose target already holds at the onset earns no credit from what was done before it: it is credited only after the pilot has made it false and true again at or after the onset, so a run that does nothing after the onset fails like on the other levels. The debrief adds "Steps done before the onset do not count." to the missed-steps line. Harmful actions are still recorded at any time. Recordings without an onset (Easy, Medium) are unaffected.
13. **Variants (R13).** A scenario file can declare variants, several causes behind one failure message (see "Variants for authors"). A run draws one by seed on every level, and the recording names it. `grade` resolves the recorded variant into a plain scenario first (the cause's faulted controls and parameters laid over, only the steps and forbidden items that apply in it kept) and then applies every rule above to that scenario. A step that does not apply in the drawn variant is not graded and is not missed; the debrief shows its row muted as "Not graded". A recording that names no variant grades as the first variant of the file, so every recording made before variants existed keeps its result. The result carries the variant id, and the debrief names the cause only after the run, in one line ("Cause: ..."). Nothing before the run names it.
14. **Controls set by the simulation (R14).** Some control changes are made by the aircraft, not by the pilot: the faulted controls of a variant on Realistic (set at the onset, so the start stays clean) and a trip of the aircraft's physics, for example a shorted alternator 1 that pops its circuit breaker again 2 s after the pilot pushed it in. These changes are events of the source `sim`. They are not in the recording: every replay regenerates them with the same arithmetic and `grade` merges them into the log at the place they happened. They are never harmful actions, the pilot cannot retract them (a retract of the pilot's tap before the delay leaves nothing armed), and a step is credited from the state after them like after any other action.
15. **The 30-minute cap (R15).** A run ends at 30 minutes of run time (`MAX_RUN_MS`) if no outcome and no declaration ended it earlier. The live run and the replay share the cap: the run completes there, is graded as of the cap by the same rules, and the debrief adds one line, "The run reached the 30 minute limit." It is a normal graded ending, not a separate fail reason; a run that did everything required passes at the cap.

16. **Decisions (R16).** The further actions are the decisions of a situation that are not panel actions: inform ATC (MAYDAY or PAN PAN PAN), brief passengers and the choice of the further flight conduct. The list is fixed by the aircraft (`src/aircraft/da50rg/further.ts`); a scenario does not add to it. They are nine toggle buttons in the pilot footwell (six since plan 07-05, nine since plan 07-06; a dialog was used before), under the headline FURTHER ACTIONS: the top row holds the three flight conducts (Continue to destination at the top left, then the alternate landing, then the emergency landing), the middle row Brief passengers, ATC: MAYDAY and ATC notify (PAN PAN PAN), the bottom row the three Avoid actions (R21). **A press is a switch** and every press is one recorded entry `{ t, id, on }` with the run time, in the order made, in the recording field `decisions` (beside the control events, which stay as they are); `on` is optional and absent means on, so every recording made before the toggles stays valid and grades byte-identically, and only the value `false` switches off (any other value reads as on). MAYDAY, PAN PAN PAN and BRIEF PASSENGERS are independent toggles, any combination can be selected. **The state at the end counts:** an action is done when its last entry is on; the one helper `effectiveSelection` (`src/engine/further.ts`; it is told whether the scenario offers a conduct) turns the entries into the selected actions and the selected conduct and is used by the grade, the session and the buttons, so the highlight and the verdict cannot disagree. The selections are graded together with the panel actions when the user presses Procedure complete; a selection never ends the run and the run clock does not wait for it. A press before the onset of a Realistic run is ignored (the buttons look the same before and after it, so the onset is not given away), a decision after the time of a named outcome (R10) is not graded, like an action after it, and a recording that names an option the aircraft does not have is a programming error and is refused with an exception, like an unknown control (R9), also for an off entry. A recording without `decisions` means none were made. The level and the checklist choice are not inputs.
17. **Roles and the verdict (R17).** A scenario can declare in an optional `further` section the role of each action: `required`, `recommended`, `optional` or `harmful`; an action that is not listed is `optional`. A required action that was not picked fails the run like a missed critical step (reason `further_missed`, with the count). A recommended action is shown in the debrief and never changes the verdict. An optional action is shown and never changes the verdict. A harmful action that was picked is shown and fails the run only when the entry says `fails_run: true`, like a forbidden item (reason `further_harmful`). Brief passengers is never `required` and never fails a run: the aircraft data marks it `unrated` and lint rule L27 rejects the roles `required` and `harmful` for it. The reasons of a verdict are in the order: the named outcome, the missed critical steps, the missed required further actions, the flight decision, the order violations, the forbidden items that fail the run, the harmful further actions that fail the run. A scenario without the section grades no further action: whatever is picked is shown as optional and the verdict is as before.
18. **The flight conduct (R18).** A scenario that has a `further` section declares `conduct` explicitly, either `none` or `accept`, a list of the conduct options that are right. The three conduct buttons are a radio group that always has exactly one selection where a conduct is offered. **Continue to destination is preselected when a run starts** (owner decision 2026-11-02; `defaultConduct` in the aircraft data): it is shown highlighted without any press, no entry is logged for it, and the pilot deviates by pressing another conduct button. Pressing another switches the selection; pressing the selected one is no change and logs nothing, so a conduct can no longer be cleared. **The preselected default counts like a pick:** `effectiveSelection` starts the conduct at the default and `gradeFurther` grades the selection at the end, so a scenario whose `accept` list lacks Continue to destination (every shipped flight scenario) fails with the reason `conduct`, status `wrong`, when the pilot never switches; the old status `missing` no longer exists (a conduct that is offered always has a selection). The final selection counts, so a switch after a first choice is graded by where it ended; the result keeps the selections in the order made (`picks`, which starts with the default) for the debrief, and `last` is the selection at the end. A recording made while a conduct could still be cleared stays readable: an `on: false` entry of the selected option falls back to the default (it graded as missing before, it now grades as wrong unless the default is accepted); no shipped recording other than the `fire-conduct-cleared` case below is affected. With `none` there is no conduct decision in that scenario: the three conduct buttons are not drawn at all (the buttons are absent, not greyed; the top row and the Avoid row stay empty and the middle row stays where it is, so no button moves between scenarios), nothing is preselected, a press of one is ignored, its absence never fails and an entry that somehow exists is shown and not graded. A scenario on the ground (the aircraft's ground parameter above 0 in its flight profile: `ground_roll`) must declare `none`, every other scenario with the section must declare `accept` and must not list every conduct option (lint rule L28). A scenario without the section has no conduct decision: the conduct buttons are absent and the verdict does not look at it.
19. **The debrief and the follow-on note (R19).** The debrief shows a section "Further actions": one row for every declared or picked action, in the order of the aircraft's list, with its role (Required, Recommended, Optional, Harmful) and its verdict (Done, Missed, Recommended and not done, Not done, Harmful), and for a scenario with `accept` the flight decision with the verdict Right or Wrong and the picks line, which starts with the preselected default ("Picked: Continue to destination" when the pilot never switched, otherwise "Picks in order: ..., then ..."); an action that was selected and cleared reads like one never selected. When the final conduct selection is the emergency landing the debrief adds one note from the aircraft data ("not trained yet") only where the scenario names no follow-on for it; since Phase 9 ENGINE FIRE IN FLIGHT and T/O FIRE NO ABORT lead into the landing situation by a button instead (rule R23). The reason lines are "N required further actions were missed.", "The flight decision was not the right one." and the line of the harmful actions.
20. **The conduct note (R20).** A scenario may carry `conduct_note` in its `further` section: own words, at most 50 words. The debrief shows it as a hint outside the verdict, never changing PASS or FAIL, when the conduct selected at the end is the one the aircraft names as `noteConduct` (Continue to destination) and the scenario has a note. The hint is yellow (the standard tone of the outline) and holds the note and, when not every Avoid action was selected at the end, the line "Not chosen: Avoid IMC, Avoid NVFR, Avoid icing." with the texts of the missing ones; when all three were selected the hint is a green line instead ("You also chose Avoid IMC, Avoid NVFR and Avoid icing, which fits continuing under VFR.", tone done, built from the texts). Another final conduct, a scenario without a note, or conduct none shows nothing. Lint L31 allows a `conduct_note` only where `accept` lists the note conduct (and never with `conduct: none`). ALTN 1 FAIL and ALTN 2 FAIL carry the note that continuing is fine under VFR only and that in IMC, at night under NVFR or in icing conditions an alternate landing is necessary. The pure function is `conductNote` in `src/ui/debrief-text.ts`.
21. **The Avoid actions and the accept lists (R21).** Avoid IMC, Avoid NVFR and Avoid icing are three more actions of the aircraft list, marked `unrated` and `avoid` in the aircraft data: independent toggles, recorded like every toggle, never required and never harmful (lint L27 rejects both roles), drawn only where the scenario offers a flight conduct (the row stays empty on the ground and without `accept`, a press there is ignored) and listed in the debrief only when picked (role Optional). Picking them changes no verdict and no reason; the result gains an entry per picked Avoid action and nothing else (a unit case compares the grade with and without the picks). Where the printed checklist allows continued VFR flight the scenario accepts both Continue to destination and the alternate landing: ALTN 1 FAIL and ALTN 2 FAIL changed to `accept` of both on purpose in plan 07-06 (their printed comment rows end NVFR/IFR flight and say to land at the nearest suitable airfield), so a run that never touches the conduct buttons now has the right conduct; the golden expectations of ALTN 1 FAIL that this changes (`elec-do-nothing` and `elec-do-nothing-long` no longer carry the reason `conduct`) are changed on purpose. Under the emergency landing Avoid NVFR and Avoid icing are greyed out in the footwell (owner decision 2026-11-03; aircraft data `blockedByConduct` of those two options, rule `isBlocked` in `src/engine/further.ts`): a press logs nothing, picks made earlier stay in the log and in the result (grade and verdict unchanged), and the debrief lists no row for them while the final conduct is the emergency landing; they show again when the conduct is switched back. Every other declaration was reviewed and kept; the review table is in `docs/PANEL-REVIEW.md` ("Phase 7: owner review").
22. **The finish (R22).** A scenario may declare an optional `finish` with a predicate `when` (the same vocabulary as an outcome) and an own-words `text` of at most 12 words. In the first simulation step in which the predicate holds, the run completes by itself, exactly as if the pilot had pressed Procedure complete at that time: `grade` is unchanged, the recording's `completedAt` is the finish time, and a golden recording stays byte-identical. The pilot may press Procedure complete earlier. When a named outcome and the finish come in one step, the outcome wins (outcomes are checked first and the finish only when none fired). There is no touchdown outcome, no crash and no damage: a pilot who did not prepare fails through the missed critical steps, a prepared pilot passes. The debrief prints the `finish` text as one line when the run ended that way. Lint L32 rejects a `finish` whose predicate does not resolve, that already holds at the start (Easy, Medium or Realistic) or that a run doing nothing does not reach within 600 s, so a situation cannot hang until the 30 minute limit.
23. **The follow-on (R23).** A scenario may name, inside `further`, a `follow_on` map from a flight conduct option to a pack and a scenario. When the conduct selected at the end of the run (the value the conduct row of the debrief shows) is a key of the map, the debrief offers a button between Retry and Next situation, with the label and title the aircraft data gives that conduct (Emergency landing, Continue with emergency landing). Pressing it opens the target through the pack seam, the same start as Next situation: the briefing on Easy and Medium, the level kept, nothing carried over from the first run (the panel starts at the baseline of the target). Each situation is graded on its own: the first run's result, recording and debrief are untouched by the offer and by what follows. The button is offered after FAIL as well, because the decision to land was made either way. It is not offered where the scenario names no follow-on for the selected conduct, and then the old note of the conduct ("not trained yet") still shows; with a button the note does not show. A Realistic run shows the level card at the target when the target declares no onset. Retry inside the follow-on repeats the follow-on. A target in a pack the access policy denies, or a file that cannot be loaded, shows the existing load-failure card, and its retry repeats the start. Lint L33 (per scenario) and L34 (pack level, run by `npm run validate`) check the declaration.

Further notes:

- A step that is already true at the starting point would be credited by a run that does nothing. The file check of the validation command rejects such a step (lint rule L5). It also rejects a file in which no critical step is graded at the start (lint rule L11). So a run that does nothing always fails in a shipped scenario.
- A tap that was taken back with the double tap gesture is removed from the log before grading, so retracted actions do not exist for the grader.

## Variants for authors

A variant is one cause behind the failure message the scenario shows. The checklist stays the one printed checklist; the variants say which of its rows are graded for which cause. The format stays 1 (the fields are new and optional, a file without `variants` is unchanged). This section is written so the Phase 7 authoring guide can lift it.

```
variants[2]:
  - id: alternator_dead
    weight: 60
    applies[2]: alt1_off,ess_bus_on
    debrief: The breaker was in and alternator 1 had failed.
  - id: breaker_popped
    weight: 40
    controls:
      cb.alt1: out
    parameters:
      alt1_output: 1
    applies[1]: alt1_breaker_in
    debrief: The alternator 1 circuit breaker had popped. Pushing it in cleared the message.
```

- **`id` and `weight`.** The id is unique in the file. The weight is a whole number from 1 to 100; a run draws a variant by seed, in proportion to the weights, on every level. At most 6 variants per scenario.
- **The first variant is the default.** It is the cause a recording without a variant is graded as (R13), and the one the shipped tests treat as the base case. Put the common cause first.
- **`controls` and `parameters`.** The faulted state of the cause: control positions (a legal position of a known control) and physics parameters (known, inside their range) laid over the scenario. On Easy and Medium they hold from the start. On Realistic the controls are set by the simulation at the onset and the start is clean; a scenario may also list controls under `onset.controls` for the same purpose without a variant.
- **`applies`.** The ids of the steps and forbidden items that apply in this variant. An item named by no variant applies in all of them; an item named by at least one applies only in those. Every id must exist, and appears once per variant.
- **`debrief`.** The cause line, 1 to 30 words in own wording. The debrief prints it after the run as "Cause: ..."; nothing names the cause before. The printed checklist text of the briefing and the debrief stays exactly as printed in every variant.
- **Re-tripping and other aircraft behaviour** is not written in the scenario file. A parameter such as `alt1_short` switches on an effect in the aircraft's physics (R14); the file only names the parameter.

The check `npm run validate` adds these rules for a scenario with variants (each is reported with file, line and column):

- L18: variant ids are unique; controls, states and parameters exist and are legal; `applies` names real steps and forbidden items; no branch decided at the start reads a control a variant sets.
- L19: the start checks of the single-cause scenario (L5, L6, L10, L11, L13, L14, L15) run for every variant, on Easy and, with an onset, on Realistic.
- L20: in every variant a run that does nothing fails and the run that does exactly the applying steps passes, on Easy and, with an onset, on Realistic at both ends of the onset window.
- L21: a step that applies in one variant must not trigger a forbidden item of another variant at its start.

## Scenes and cues for authors (phase 5)

The windscreen above the instrument panel shows what the pilot sees outside, and it shows the visible side of a failure. Both are data. The format stays 1; the fields are optional, a file without them is unchanged. This section is written so the Phase 7 authoring guide can lift it.

```
scene: cruise
onset:
  window_s[2]: 2,6
  cues[1]: flames
```

- **`scene`.** The view out of the windscreen: `parked` (in front of a hangar), `takeoff_roll` (a runway; its marks scroll), `cruise` (sky above, simple landscape below), `cloud` (flight in cloud, for icing) or `outlanding` (the cruise landscape with one landing field ahead that grows with the descent, Phase 9). The default is `cruise`, and it is what a scenario without the field gets and what the free panel shows. A scene is shown from the briefing on and during the run. L22 rejects an id that is not a scene of the aircraft. The scenes are art; no text is in them.
- **`onset.cues`.** The visible cues the failure shows at its onset, at most four ids. A cue is a simulated quantity read as a level from 0 to 1; it is drawn when the level is at least 0.1 (`CUE_VISIBLE_LEVEL`). The cue table is aircraft data (`src/aircraft/da50rg/cues.ts`); the engine only reads levels (`src/engine/cues.ts`). On Easy and Medium the cue follows the scenario's start quantities, so a fire that starts at 40 shows its flames at once. A cue is never feedback on right or wrong: the level is not an input of `grade`.

| Cue id             | Quantity         | Level 0 at | Level 1 at | What shows                                                 |
| ------------------ | ---------------- | ---------- | ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| `flames`           | `fire_intensity` | 0          | 60         | flames and a smoke trail over the cowl, right of the strut |
| `cabin_smoke`      | `smoke_density`  | 0          | 70         | smoke over the instrument panel; it never takes a press    |
| `ice_pilot_side`   | `ice_pilot`      | 0          | 60         | frost and crystals on the pilot half of the windscreen     |
| `ice_copilot_side` | `ice_copilot`    | 0          | 60         | the same on the co-pilot half                              |
| `prop_stopped`     | `rpm`            | 100        | 99         | two states: a faint disc while it turns, a crisp blade     |
| `oil_streaks`      | `oil_film`       | 0          | 50         | oil streaks running back over the glass                    |
| `field_near`       | `altitude_ft`    | 2600       | 1400       | the landing field of the scene `outlanding` grows (Phase 9) |

- **The landing field.** `field_near` is a falling cue: its level is 0 at the terrain height plus 1200 ft (`FIELD_SIGHT_FT`, an instructor-review value; 2600 ft at the normal terrain) and 1 at the terrain height (`TERRAIN_HEIGHT_FT`, 1400 ft). The windscreen and the PFD synthetic vision read the same level through one geometry function, so they cannot disagree. A scenario on the scene `outlanding` keeps the normal terrain, so the cue and the physics share one height. It needs no onset: it is part of the view, not of a failure.
- **Parameters.** A scenario switches a hidden effect on through a parameter, like `oil_cooling`: `smoke_source` (0 to 1, normal 0: the cabin fills with smoke), `ice_accretion` (0 to 1, normal 0: the windscreen ices over) and `oil_leak` (0 to 1, normal 0: the glass smears while the engine runs). `engine_fire` drives the flames through `fire_intensity`. Since Phase 5.1 the pack `fire` uses `smoke_source`; no shipped scenario uses the other two yet; the preview flags (below) and the fixture `tests/fixtures/scenarios/smoke-onset.toon` exercise them.
- **Preview flags.** On the free panel `?scene=<id>` sets the scene, `?q=<quantity>:<value>,...` sets start quantities (for example `?q=smoke_density:80`) and `?flags=<flag>,...` sets a reaction flag (for example `deice.windshield_spray`). Unknown names and out-of-range values are ignored. RESET clears them.

The check `npm run validate` adds three rules (each reported with file, line and column):

- L22: `scene` names a scene of the aircraft. A failing file reads like `scenarios/x/y.toon:3:1: unknown scene "night"; known scenes: parked, takeoff_roll, cruise, cloud, outlanding`.
- L23: every cue of `onset.cues` is a cue of the aircraft and is listed once, it is not visible at the Realistic start, and a do-nothing Realistic run shows it within the bound (12 s, or the scenario's own `message_within_s`) at both ends of the onset window. A failing file reads like `scenarios/x/y.toon:12:7: the cue "flames" is already visible at the realistic start; the level Realistic must begin without a cue`.

- L25: the flight `profile` (see "Flight profile for authors (phase 6)"): every key a profile parameter inside its range, no profile parameter under `parameters`, no key in the profile and again in a later layer, no start value below a floor parameter.
- L26 (phase 6 review, WR-01): a run that does nothing shows no message the file does not declare. A message that becomes active after the start must be listed under `annunciations` (shown at the start, L10; or shown within the bound after the onset, L15) or under the optional `late_annunciations` (format 1, default empty): `"stall_heat_ground" becomes active 5.1 s into a run that does nothing (Easy and Medium) but is not listed; list it under late_annunciations`. ## Further actions for authors (phase 7)

The further actions are the decisions beyond the panel (R16 to R19). The format stays 1; the `further` section is optional, a file without it grades exactly as before; the footwell then shows the calls and the passengers unrated and no flight conduct buttons. A scenario with `accept` that does not list `conduct_destination` makes the pilot switch away from the preselected Continue to destination; listing it makes the untouched default right, which the printed checklist must allow (the two ALTN cautions do, R20 and R21). The aircraft owns the list of options (`src/aircraft/da50rg/further.ts`); a scenario only says what each is worth in its situation. This section is written so the README guide lifts it.

```toon
# The roles are instructor-review values.
further:
  actions[3]:
    - id: atc_mayday
      role: required
    - id: atc_pan_pan
      role: optional
    - id: brief_passengers
      role: recommended
  conduct:
    accept[1]: conduct_emergency
```

A scenario that accepts continuing to the destination can add a note for the debrief (own words, at most 50 words, `conduct_note` inside the section after `conduct`):

```toon fragment
  conduct:
    accept[2]: conduct_destination,conduct_precautionary
  conduct_note: "Continuing is fine under VFR only. In IMC, at night under NVFR or in icing conditions an alternate landing is necessary."
```

- **`actions`.** A list of `id` and `role` (`required`, `recommended`, `optional` or `harmful`), each action once; `fails_run: true` is allowed on `harmful` only. The options are `atc_mayday`, `atc_pan_pan`, `brief_passengers` and the three Avoid actions `avoid_imc`, `avoid_nvfr`, `avoid_icing`, which are never required or harmful. An action that is not listed is `optional`.
- **`conduct`.** Required inside the section. `accept` lists the conduct options that are right (`conduct_destination`, `conduct_precautionary`, `conduct_emergency`; at least one and never all three); the selection at the end of the run must be one of them. A scenario on the ground or on a landing scene (the scene `outlanding`, Phase 9) writes `conduct: none` on one line. `conduct_note` is optional and belongs to a scenario whose `accept` lists `conduct_destination` (L31).
- **Where the section goes.** Last in the file, after the other blocks, with a comment line that says the roles are instructor-review values (the owner lists them in `docs/PANEL-REVIEW.md`).
- **Defaults of the shipped files.** A caution you can handle in flight: all three actions optional and a precautionary landing (ENG OIL TEMP, ICING CONDITIONS), or continuing to the destination and the precautionary landing with the conduct note (ALTN 1 FAIL, ALTN 2 FAIL). A fire in flight (ENGINE FIRE, ELEC FIRE FLIGHT, T/O FIRE NO ABORT): MAYDAY required, brief passengers recommended; the right conduct is the emergency landing for ENGINE FIRE and T/O FIRE NO ABORT (the printed list stops the engine) and the precautionary (alternate) landing for ELEC FIRE FLIGHT (the engine keeps running, owner answer 2026-11-03). A fire on the ground (ENGINE FIRE GROUND, ELEC FIRE GROUND, T/O FIRE ABORT): MAYDAY recommended and `conduct: none`.

The check `npm run validate` adds three rules for the section, two for the catalog and two for the follow-on (each is reported with file, line and column):

- L27: every `actions` id is an action option of the aircraft and listed once (a conduct id in the list is reported as such); `fails_run` only on the role `harmful`; an `unrated` action of the aircraft (brief passengers) is never `required` or `harmful`.
- L33 and L34 (Phase 9, see "Emergency landing situations for authors"): a `follow_on` key is a flight conduct the aircraft gives a button, which `accept` lists, and never with `conduct: none` (L33); the target names a manifest entry of a checked pack and not the scenario itself (L34, pack level).
- L31: a `conduct_note` only where `accept` lists the note conduct of the aircraft (Continue to destination), never with `conduct: none`; at most 50 words (the schema).
- L29 and L30 (pack level, not per scenario): every shipped scenario is the `ref` of exactly one entry of the checklist catalog (`src/aircraft/da50rg/checklist-catalog.ts`, the contents pages of the booklet in three groups) and every `ref` names a scenario of a checked pack; the tag of a linked entry equals the `checklist.heading` of its scenario. A new scenario therefore needs an entry in the catalog (a `ref` on the entry with its printed tag, or a new entry in the group of the non-annunciated procedures); without it `npm run validate` prints `L29: scenario "pack/id" is not the link of any entry of the checklist catalog`. At run time a scenario the catalog does not know is listed after the entries of the last group, so it stays reachable; the check is not a per-open lint, so a paid pack does not need an entry in the public catalog.
- L28: `conduct` is `none` exactly when the profile sets the ground parameter above 0 or the scenario's scene is a landing scene of the aircraft (`landingScenes` in `src/aircraft/da50rg/further.ts`, today `outlanding`: the decision to land is made, so no flight conduct is asked again). A ground or landing-scene scenario must say none, and the message names the reason (`ground scenario: ...` or `landing scene "outlanding": ...`); any other scenario with the section must say accept; every `accept` id is a conduct option of the aircraft and listed once; `accept` must not list every conduct option.

The L20 solution of a scenario with variants also makes the required decisions and the first accepted conduct, so the solution of every variant still passes.

The buttons carry the static labels Brief passengers (if time permits), ATC: MAYDAY, ATC notify (PAN PAN PAN), Avoid IMC, Avoid NVFR, Avoid icing, CONTINUE TO / DESTINATION, ALTERNATE / LANDING and EMERGENCY / LANDING (aircraft data, `label` of each option; the longer `text` is the debrief row). ALTERNATE LANDING is the label of the precautionary landing option `conduct_precautionary`.

**The sheet and the choosers (plan 07-06).** CHOOSE SITUATION lists the situations in three tabs, the three printed groups of the catalog, with three rows per page; the Realistic checklist chooser shows the same groups as tabs with the full printed headings and every catalog entry, greyed where no scenario is shipped. The order of the lists (and of Next situation) is the catalog order. None of this is an input of `grade`. The checklist sheet marks the steps of the shown checklist from the pilot's real actions; rows the program does not check (every plain action line, and the panel steps while no mark is shown or when panel state cannot decide them) are 44 px buttons the user can tick, UI state only (see "What goes in").

## Retry and next situation (phase 7)

The debrief offers two ways on, and a third where the scenario names a follow-on. None is an input of `grade`; both start a normal run through the same code as a first start, so the baseline check and the replay rules apply unchanged.

- **Retry** starts the same scenario again from a clean state (the panel at the scenario's baseline, the quantities reset, a new clock, an empty log, no decisions) at the same level. The seed rule is one function, `retrySeeds(onset, variant)` in `src/ui/session.ts`: a retry reuses the records of the graded run as recorded, the variant with its seed on every level that has variants and the onset with its seed on Realistic, and it draws nothing, so a better result comes from the pilot and not from the draw. The alternative rule (a new draw unless the run was Realistic) is a change of that one function and of its test. A normal start draws the onset seed first and the variant seed second, as before, so no recorded run changes.
- **Next situation** opens the entry after the current one in the listing of CHOOSE SITUATION (every allowed pack in manifest order, wrapping after the last; a single entry gives the same one), leaves the debrief like RESET does and keeps the level. Easy and Medium show the briefing, Realistic starts the run at once when the scenario declares an onset and shows the level card when it does not. It draws new seeds as every start does.
- **Continue with the follow-on** (Phase 9, rule R23) is a third button, shown only when the scenario names a follow-on for the conduct selected at the end. It opens the named scenario exactly as Next situation opens its entry (same level, nothing carried over) and Retry inside it repeats that follow-on.

Both buttons are ignored outside the debrief and while a load runs.

## Instructor review (phase 3, 2026-10-23)

Phase 3 made the quantities move and added two scenarios. 1. **Physics constants (A1).** The models are first-order lags toward a target, written as data in `src/aircraft/da50rg/physics.ts`. Time constants: oil temperature 60 s, coolant 40 s, gearbox 60 s, fuel temperature 60 s, engine speed 2 s, load 1 s, oil pressure 2 s, fuel pressure 1 s, alternator currents 1.5 s, bus voltage 1 s, fire intensity 30 s. The cruise base values are the equilibrium of every model (oil temperature 91 degrees C, speed 1995 rpm, load 70 percent, oil pressure 5 bar, coolant 85, gearbox 75, fuel temperature 20, fuel pressure 0.4 bar, 20 gallons per tank, 28 V, alternator 1 at 30 A and alternator 2 at 10 A, battery 100 percent). The time constants are compressed (a few seconds to tens of seconds, against minutes in the real engine) so that a situation resolves in one to five minutes: on Easy and Medium an ignored ENG OIL TEMP run ends in the named outcome after 173.2 s (the oil temperature starts at 128 degrees, already in red, and needs about 158 s to reach 140 degrees, then holds 15 s). This is the owner's retest of 2026-10-23 (it was 42.6 s): the pilot needs time to get the checklist out, and the real oil temperature does not rise that fast. The oil temperature has a rate that depends on its own reading: a rising oil temperature follows its target at a multiple of the 60 s time constant read from the curve `OIL_TEMP_RISE_RATE_CURVE` in `src/aircraft/da50rg/oil-temp-curve.ts` (points as degrees C and rate: 100 1, 112 3, 115 3, 116 0.75, 125 0.75, 128 0.175, 150 0.175), so it is fast just below the yellow band, moderate in the yellow band and slow in and above the red band. A falling oil temperature uses the plain lag (rate 1, `OIL_TEMP_RECOVERY_RATE` in `physics.ts`). The oil temperature carries a finer scale (0.0001) than the other readings, because at the slow rate a step moves it by less than 0.01. The old aircraft parameter `oil_temp_rate` (1 to 10, set to 3 by the onset block) is gone; the Realistic run differs from Easy only by its start reading and the onset. ## Fuel model (phase 6)

The fuel system is a model in `src/aircraft/da50rg/physics.ts`, with the quantities in `quantities.ts` and the calculator in `fuel-calculator.ts`. Everything is derived from the simulated engine state with `+ - * /` only, so a live run and its replay agree to the last digit; the free panel stays static. The rules do not change grading: no graded predicate reads these quantities yet, and `tests/fixtures/recordings.json` is unchanged. The values the owner gave are marked (owner); every other number is an instructor-review value and a data edit in the file named.

- **Tanks and the selector (owner, 2026-10-28).** Two tanks of 26 US gallons (`TANK_CAPACITY_GAL`, both quantities run 0 to 26 and share one band table: red to 1, green to 26). The FUEL selector picks the tank the engine draws from: NORMAL the left tank, EMERGENCY the right tank, OFF none.
- **Supply, burn and return.** The pump supplies a constant `FUEL_SUPPLY_GPH` to the engine rail while the engine runs. The engine burns the fuel flow (the fitted line of item 1 of the phase 3 review); the rest returns to the LEFT tank, whatever the selector says, as long as the left tank is below its capacity (the left-full rule: with the left tank full the return is 0). The drawn tank loses burn plus return, the left tank gains the return. So NORMAL leaves the right tank untouched and the left tank at minus the burn; EMERGENCY empties the right tank at the supply flow, more than the displayed fuel flow, while the left tank gains supply less burn; the two tanks together always lose exactly the burn. A leak is not built (it is for the later fuel situations); the rate of each tank is one function, so a leak parameter adds one term.
- **Starvation.** An empty drawn tank (quantity 0) cuts the fuel like the selector OFF: the engine stops and every consumer follows (the fire feed, the pump pressure, the alternators). The engine comes back at once when fuel is available again; no relight procedure is modelled (the empty-tank situation of a later phase adds it).
- **Transfer (owner).** FUEL TRANS pumps `FUEL_TRANSFER_PER_MIN` = 1 gal/min, which is the 60 US gal/h of the owner, from the right tank into the left one while the knob is on, the right tank is above 0 and the left tank is below 26. The pump does not read the electrical state (the pump bus and its breaker are not modelled; the breaker-effects todo keeps that item). The rates of the transfer and the EMERGENCY flow add; the clamp holds each tank inside 0 to 26.
- **The white FUEL TRANS message (owner).** A new CAS level `advisory`: white, listed after the warnings and cautions, and it never sounds a tone. It is a band rule like the alternator faults: the predicate vocabulary has no AND, so physics sets the hidden quantity `fuel_trans_active` (0 or 1, one step behind the transfer rate) from the same condition as the transfer, and the rule `fuel_trans` reads its band `active`. The message and the pump cannot disagree, and no number enters the rule table.
- **Fuel temperatures (owner).** Left = outside air temperature + `FUEL_TEMP_LH_OFFSET_C` (8), right = left - delta. The outside air temperature is the parameter `oat_c` (-40 to 50, normal 15) and the delta the parameter `fuel_temp_delta_c` (-10 to 10, normal 3; the owner range in the normal situation is 2 to 5): a situation sets both through its `profile` block (plan 06-03: in force from the first second; `parameters` still works and starts at the onset), and a negative delta reverses the tanks (the hot return runs through the right tank and pre-warms it). Both quantities follow their target with a 60 s lag; the oil, coolant and gearbox engine-off targets follow `oat_c` too and are unchanged at the normal value. The limits are -25 and +60 degrees C (owner): the red zones start there and the quantity range (-30 to 70) is drawn wider so the red ends show; the model holds each derived target to the limits (read from the band tops of the quantity data), so a derived reading never enters red by itself, while a start quantity of a situation can.
- **Fuel flow and gallons used.** `fuel_flow_gph` follows the burn within one step (base 11.23 gph, the cruise equilibrium of the line: 10.1 gph at lever 60 percent, 11.8 gph at 75 percent, 0 with the engine stopped); `fuel_used_gal` rises by the burn (base 12, the 52 gallon capacity less the 40 gallons of the two base tanks). Refuelling and the RST Fuel softkey are not modelled.
- **The calculator** (`fuelCalculator`): remain = left + right tank; used = `fuel_used_gal`; endurance in whole minutes (truncated) = remain over the flow times 60, none while the flow is below `ENDURANCE_MIN_FLOW_GPH` (1) or the result would exceed `ENDURANCE_MAX_MIN` (99 h 59 min), printed H:MM or --:--; range in nautical miles = the exact endurance in hours times `airspeed_kt` (no wind, so 0 on the apron). It has no quantity of its own.
- **Instructor-review constants.** Supply 30 gph (above the burn at full POWER, 14.6 gph, so the return is never negative); the left offset of 8 degrees; the default delta of 3 degrees (owner range 2 to 5); the 1 gph endurance floor; the 12 gallons of the base of the gallons used; the 60 s fuel temperature lag; the FUEL TRANS pump working without electrical power; the engine restarting at once when the empty tank is switched away.

## Engine page and de-ice fluid (phase 6)

The engine page of the MFD (plan 06-02) draws the quantities of the simulation; it grades nothing and no scenario step reads it. The values below are what the page shows and what an author may set or rely on.

- **The layout rule.** The page is data: `src/aircraft/da50rg/engine-page.ts` lists three columns of boxes (left: Load arc, Oil Pres, Oil Temp, Coolant Temp and Gearbox Temp bars, Rudder Trim; middle: RPM arc and the Fuel box with its Fuel Qty, Fuel Temp, Fuel Press, FFlow GPH and Fuel Calculator rows; right: Electrical, Oxygen, De-Ice with its Endurance box, Total Service), each with a rectangle in the local space of the MFD. The frame draws what is listed and holds no page coordinate. Every text is static, in capitals, and listed in `docs/PANEL-LABELS.md`; only numbers, pointers and colours move. Oil pressure is a bar; only Load and RPM are arcs.
- **Fuel Qty and Fuel Temp.** One bar each with two pointers: pointer L above the bar reads the left tank, pointer R below it the right tank, both on one scale and one band table (Fuel Qty 0 to 26 with the numerals 0, 5 to 25; Fuel Temp with the red ends beyond the limits). The Map strip shows both tanks on the same kind of bar (label FUEL QTY GAL).
- **Static display values.** `rudder_trim_pct` (-100 to 100, base 0), `oxygen_psi` (0 to 2000, base 1270, red to 250, yellow to 400, green to 1700), `service_hours` (base 249.5), `qnh_hpa` (950 to 1050, base 1013, the barometer setting of the PFD, Phase 6.1; it does not correct the altitude) and the start value of `deice_qty_gal` have no model that moves them in a run (the de-ice quantity has the fluid model below); a scenario may set them. The bases and the oxygen bands are layout assumptions, not AFM figures; they are on the owner review list.
- **Endurance of the de-ice fluid.** `deiceEndurance(quantity)` = quantity divided by the flow of the mode times 60, in whole minutes, printed H:MM: Norm at 3 gal/h, High at 6 gal/h (`DEICE_NORM_GPH`, `DEICE_HIGH_GPH`, the owner's TKS figures of 2026-10-28). At the base quantity of 6.8 gal these are 2:16 and 1:08. An empty tank reads 0:00.
- **The fluid model.** `deice_qty_gal` (tank maximum 8.3 US gal, the owner figure) falls by the flow of the de-ice mode while `sw.deice_mode` is NORM or HIGH (OFF uses nothing). One MAX activation (the 120 s run of the reaction `deice_max`, only on HIGH) takes `DEICE_MAX_ACTIVATION_GAL` = 0.4 gal in total, spread evenly over the run, in place of the HIGH flow during the run; the HIGH flow resumes after it. One WIND SHIELD press takes `DEICE_SPRAY_ML` = 40 ml (converted with `ML_PER_US_GAL` = 3785.41) spread over the 10 s of the spray, on top of the mode flow (a separate nozzle). The tank never leaves 0 to 8.3; the spray and the pilot-side protection do not depend on the fluid (an empty tank belongs with the later de-ice situations). The quantity has the fine scale of the fuel quantities, because a NORM step uses only 0.00008 gal. Open owner point: whether the 0.4 gal of a MAX activation come on top of the HIGH flow instead of replacing it (the figure in this build is the total of the 120 s run; a one-line data change).
- **ANNUN TEST.** While `sw.deice_ice_light` stands at `annun_test` the three de-ice lamps light, whatever the mode. Which lamps the real test lights is not in any source of the project; the three lamps of the system are an assumption the owner confirms. It works on the free panel (the lamps derive from control state). Whether the position springs back stays question Q-02 of `docs/PANEL-LABELS.md`.
- **Deferred, with reasons.** The ICE LIGHT effect only matters at night, and the dusk and night scenes are a deferred todo, so there is nothing to show yet. The ELT lamp in RESET and TEST: its behaviour is in no source of the project (missing information); `lt.elt` stays lit in ON only.

## Pitot and stall heat (phase 6)

Two yellow cautions on the PFD follow the PITOT/STALL HEAT rocker (`sw.pitot_stall_heat`, plan 06-04; owner description 2026-10-28, confirmations D-12). The rules are in `src/aircraft/da50rg/cas.ts`, the timer in `quantities.ts` and `physics.ts`; they grade nothing by themselves.

- **The rules.** `pitot_heat` (text PITOT HT OFF) and `stall_heat` (text STALL HT OFF) are both level caution and both state checks on the rocker: whenever it is off both show, as two independent chips in this order. `stall_heat_ground` (text STALL HT OFF, caution) is a band check on the hidden quantity `stall_heat_timer_s` in the band `cut`. The last two never hold together (the rocker off resets the timer), so at most one STALL HT OFF chip shows. A faulty system as a third cause is not built (no source describes a fault input); the electrical dependence of the heat stays in the breaker-effects todo.
- **The ground cut-out.** `stall_heat_timer_s` (hidden, 0 to 60 s, band `warm` up to `STALL_HEAT_CUTOUT_S` = 5 s, then `cut`) is a rate model: it counts one second per second while the rocker is on and the parameter `ground_roll` is above 0, and falls back to 0 within one step otherwise (in the air, or with the rocker off). So on the ground the STALL HT OFF message returns 5.1 s after the heat is on and airborne it disappears at once. The 5 s are an instructor-review value ("a few seconds", owner). There is no switch-on event: on the ground the heat counts as on from the start of the run, because a reaction on a latching control would consume the event and break the double-tap retract of the gesture contract.
- **The ground situations (owner, D-12; changed 2026-10-29).** A situation begins after the take-off checklist, in which the pilot has already set the PITOT/STALL HEAT switch ON, so on the ground the STALL HT OFF caution is already showing when the run starts; it must not pop up seconds in. Every shipped situation therefore starts with the switch ON (the registry default, nothing in `initial.controls`), except the icing situation, whose variant `heat_off` involves the switch. The three grounded situations (ENGINE FIRE GROUND, ELEC FIRE GROUND, T/O FIRE ABORT; `ground_roll` in the profile) start with the hidden cut-out timer `stall_heat_timer_s` past the cut band (60, in `initial.quantities` for Easy and Medium and in `onset.quantities` for Realistic), so the chip is in its final state from the first step at every level. The chip is part of the start state: it is listed under `annunciations` (L10) and the briefing says it is showing. In the air with the switch ON no STALL HT OFF message shows. The existing reset stays: switching the heat off clears the ground chip for the two off cautions at once, and switching it on again on the ground brings the chip back after the 5 s count (the timer is reset to 0 by the off, so the count starts over); the owner's change concerns the start only, so the re-on wait did not contradict it and was kept. A grounded file that forgets the timer start is caught by the lint (the chip is then not active at the start, L10 rejects the listed chip, or L26 rejects the late, undeclared chip).
- **Ambient messages (L14, L24).** A CAS rule can be flagged `ambient` in the aircraft data: a message of the situation, not of a failure (only `stall_heat_ground` is). L14 asks that the Realistic start gives the failure away by no message; it skips ambient messages, so a grounded Realistic start carries the STALL HT OFF chip before the onset and still shows no failure cue, and every other message at the Realistic start is still rejected. L24 does not count an ambient annunciation as something the onset reveals: the onset still needs a failure annunciation or a declared cue. L10 and L26 are unchanged (the ambient chip is a start message, listed under `annunciations`).
- **The icing situation starts with the heat off.** Its checklist asks the pilot to switch the heat on, so the start state has it off, which fires both cautions. The level Realistic must begin without a message (lint L14) and one `initial.controls` set serves every level, so the heat-off start is the single variant `heat_off` (weight 1, controls `sw.pitot_stall_heat` off, applies nothing, a debrief cause line) and `sw.pitot_stall_heat` is not in `initial.controls`: Easy and Medium start with the heat off and both messages (listed in `annunciations`); Realistic starts with the registry default (on) and the simulation switches it off at the onset, so both messages show next to the ice cues within the lint bound. A scenario with a single variant is valid; lint accepts it (L18 to L21 run per variant).
- **Free panel.** The rocker off shows the two chips on the free panel too (they derive from control state); the address flag `?q=stall_heat_timer_s:10` shows the single ground chip.

## Oil pressure (phase 6)

The oil pressure follows the oil temperature and POWER (plan 06-04; owner description 2026-10-28, D-10). The model target is the pressure at idle for the live oil temperature plus the added pressure per percent of lever for that temperature times the lever travel in percent, and 0 with the engine stopped; the reading lags it by 2 s. Both tables are in `src/aircraft/da50rg/oil-pressure-curve.ts` (`OIL_PRESSURE_IDLE_CURVE`, `OIL_PRESSURE_SLOPE_CURVE`, built with `defineCurve`, read with `interpolate`); the bands stay in the quantity data (`oil_pressure_bar`: red to 2, yellow to 3.5, green to 7, yellow to 7.5, red to 9). Only `+ - * /` and the clamp.

- **The points (instructor-review values chosen to meet the description).** Idle, oil temperature in degrees C to bar: -25 8.0, 15 7.2, 30 7.2, 50 6.2, 70 5.2, 91 4.3, 115 3.7, 125 3.45, 150 3.1. Slope in bar per percent of lever: -25 0.06, 15 0.05, 30 0.04, 50 0.03, 70 0.02, 91 0.01, 150 0.01.
- **What they give.** Oil 91 degrees C and the cruise lever (70): 5.0, the base value (C-09 holds). Hot oil (125) at the cruise lever: 4.15, in the lower third of the green band (the third ends at 4.67). Hot oil at idle: 3.45, just inside the low yellow band (allowed). A cold engine (15 to 30 degrees) at idle: 7.2, in the high yellow band; one lever step (5 percent) above idle on a cold engine adds 0.2 to 0.25 bar and from lever 25 on it reads 8.2 to 8.5, in the high red band. At -25 degrees and idle the table gives 8.0, already red: that point is outside the cold start the owner describes and is an open review point. The OIL PRES warning keeps reading the red bands: hot oil never reaches them (the lowest table value, 3.1, is above the red limit of 2), a cold engine with power does.
- **Consequence for steady states.** A higher POWER has a hotter oil, so the settled pressure falls as POWER rises (about 5.9 at lever 50 with oil at 75 degrees, 5.0 at 70, 4.7 at 100 with oil at 115) while at a fixed oil temperature it rises with POWER; `tests/oil-pressure.test.ts` therefore compares at a fixed oil temperature through the model's target function.
- **The ENG OIL TEMP situation.** It starts at oil 128 degrees C and lever 75, so its start pressure is the model value there, 4.16 bar (was 5), still in the green band: the start-decided branch "If in green range" and the recorded grades are unchanged (`tests/fixtures/recordings.json` is not modified). The file says so in a comment line. Its Realistic start leaves the pressure at the base 5 (the onset block may not set a quantity a start-decided branch reads, lint L15) and it settles toward the model value of its oil temperature (about 4.5 at 114 degrees, falling toward 4.16 as the oil heats), always green. No shipped situation starts cold.

## Emergency landing situations for authors (phase 9)

A pilot who ends a situation with the flight conduct "Initiate emergency landing" can continue from the debrief into an emergency-landing situation: a simple approach to an off-airport field, the pre-landing checklist done on the panel, graded like every situation. The format stays 1; every key below is optional and a file without it is unchanged. This section is written so the README guide can lift it.

```toon fragment
scene: outlanding
profile:
  engine_stopped: 1
  glide_speed_kt: 80
  glide_vs_fpm: -700
finish:
  when:
    check: quantity
    quantity: altitude_ft
    op: lte
    value: 1400
  text: The aircraft reached the field
further:
  conduct: none
```

- **The scene and the cue.** `scene: outlanding` shows the cruise landscape with one landing field ahead, slightly right of the strut. The field is part of the scene; the pilot does not choose it and there is one field. It grows with the cue `field_near` (see "Scenes and cues for authors") on the windscreen and on the PFD synthetic vision. Keep the normal terrain height (1400 ft) in a scenario on this scene; another terrain needs its own cue.
- **No landing model.** The descent is a script of the flight profile (`flight_speed_kt` and a negative `flight_vs_fpm` with power; `glide_speed_kt` and `glide_vs_fpm` without). The aircraft ends level on the terrain, as in Phase 6. The numbers of the shipped situations are instructor-review values.
- **Without engine power.** The profile parameter `engine_stopped` (0 to 1, normal 0) stops the engine from the first second at every level without a checklist row: rpm falls to 0, the propeller shows stopped, fuel flow is 0, and the existing glide regime applies; the electrical and CAS behaviour of a stopped engine follows from the existing models. List the messages that fire (lint L10 and L26 tell which); set the start quantities of the dead engine (`rpm`, `load_pct`, `oil_pressure_bar`, `fuel_flow_gph`, `fuel_pressure_bar`) in `initial.quantities` for a first frame that already shows it. A file without the parameter is unchanged.
- **The end.** `finish` (rule R22) ends the run by itself when the aircraft reaches the field: the predicate is `altitude_ft` at most the terrain height. `text` is a line of at most 12 words the debrief prints. L32 reports, with file, line and column: `the finish already holds in the initial state; the run would complete at once`, `the finish already holds at the realistic start; the run would complete at once`, and `the finish is not reached within 600 s of a run that does nothing; the situation would hang until the 30 minute limit`; a predicate that does not resolve is reported at `finish.when`.
- **The declarations.** A landing situation says `conduct: none` (the decision to land is made; the three conduct buttons and the Avoid row are absent). MAYDAY and brief passengers are usually `recommended` and PAN PAN PAN `optional`; none of them can fail a run. Lint L28 enforces `none` on a landing scene (see "Further actions for authors").
- **The follow-on from a situation that ends in a landing** (rule R23): inside its `further` section the source scenario writes the target after `conduct`:

```toon fragment
further:
  conduct:
    accept[1]: conduct_emergency
  follow_on:
    conduct_emergency:
      pack: landing
      scenario: landing-engine-off
```

  `pack` and `scenario` are the pack folder id and the manifest entry id; they are opened only through the pack seam, never through a path. L33 (per scenario, reported at `further.follow_on.<id>`) reports a key that is not a conduct the aircraft gives a button (`known: ...`), a conduct `accept` does not list, and a follow-on under `conduct: none`. L34 (pack level, with L29 and L30, run by `npm run validate`) reports a target that is not a manifest entry of a checked pack and a target that is the scenario itself; a target in a pack that was not checked is not judged, so a paid pack needs no entry in the public data.
- **The note.** The debrief shows the note of the emergency landing ("not trained yet") only where it offers no button for the final conduct, so it stays true for a scenario without a follow-on.

The pack `landing` holds one situation, LANDING ENGINE OFF, the booklet page 9 EMERGENCY LANDING (ENGINE OFF), typed as printed (heading, rows 1 to 15 with their actions, the comment line "On final / before touchdown:" before rows 12 to 14, and the boxed table "Approach speed KIAS" after row 14 as four boxed comment rows, row 15 after the table). ## Flight profile for authors (phase 6)

The PFD is live inside a situation: the horizon pitches and rolls, the speed and altitude tapes slide and the numbers change (heading stays static). It draws simulated quantities, and a scenario scripts them in data. No aerodynamic model is behind it (owner decisions D-08 and D-09): the situation is a script, deterministic and replayable, and it grades nothing (no predicate reads these quantities and `tests/fixtures/recordings.json` is unchanged). The free panel keeps its static readings (140 kt, 1400 ft, 0 fpm, level). The format stays 1; the section is optional and a file without it is unchanged. This section is written so the Phase 7 authoring guide can lift it.

```
initial:
  quantities:
    altitude_ft: 1450
profile:
  flight_speed_kt: 70
  flight_vs_fpm: 500
onset:
  window_s[2]: 2,6
  quantities:
    altitude_ft: 1450
```

- **`profile` next to `parameters`.** `parameters` are in force from the onset (the failure). `profile` holds the parameters of the flight situation itself, in force from the first second at every level, before and after the onset, so a climbing take-off does not drift to cruise speed while the Realistic onset is still two to six seconds away. Order of layers: the aircraft's normal values, the profile, the scenario's `parameters`, the parameters of the onset block. A scenario without a profile gives exactly the old parameter sets.
- **Which keys qualify.** Only parameters the aircraft data flags `profile: true`; lint rule L25 reports any other key at `profile.<id>` (a failure parameter such as `oil_cooling` belongs under `parameters`) and a value outside the range. The flagged parameters, taken from `src/aircraft/da50rg/physics.ts`: `ground_roll` (0 or 1, normal 0; the aircraft is on the ground, rolling or parked), `engine_stopped` (0 to 1, normal 0; above 0 the engine is stopped from the first second at every level, Phase 9), `flight_speed_kt` (0 to 200, normal 140), `flight_vs_fpm` (-2000 to 2000, normal 0), `flight_bank_deg` (-60 to 60, normal 0), `glide_speed_kt` (0 to 200, normal 90), `glide_vs_fpm` (-2000 to 2000, normal -700), `terrain_ft` (0 to 10000, normal 1400), and from the fuel model `oat_c` and `fuel_temp_delta_c` (a reversed fuel temperature difference is situation data, so it belongs here and is in force from the first second).
- **Start values.** Altitude and speed start in `initial.quantities` (Easy and Medium) and, for the Realistic start, in `onset.quantities`: the Realistic start is the base values laid over by the onset quantities, never the initial ones, so a start altitude or speed written only in `initial` would be lost there. That is why `altitude_ft` and, where the aircraft already moves, `airspeed_kt` appear in both blocks.
- **The three regimes.** (1) Ground: the parameter `ground_roll` is above 0 (set in the profile of the ground situations, from the first second): the airspeed follows the ground speed within one step, as since phase 5.1, and the vertical speed, pitch and bank are 0 and the altitude holds. (2) Powered flight: the engine runs, and the targets are `flight_speed_kt`, `flight_vs_fpm` and `flight_bank_deg`. (3) Engine stopped (ENGINE MASTER off, the FUEL selector OFF, an empty drawn tank, or the profile parameter `engine_stopped` above 0): the targets are `glide_speed_kt` and `glide_vs_fpm`; the bank stays. Nothing else moves them, in particular not the POWER lever.
- **The glide defaults and how to override them.** An aircraft default applies to every scenario: 90 kt and -700 fpm. A scenario overrides them in its profile (`glide_speed_kt`, `glide_vs_fpm`); a situation that stands or rolls sets both to 0 so a stopped engine changes nothing.
- **The ground.** The altitude never sinks below `terrain_ft` (normal 1400): a descent ends on it and its vertical speed is held at 0 there. The simulation has no landing model; a glide ends level at the terrain height with the speed it had (the emergency landing of Phase 9 has no landing model either: no flare, no touchdown speed, no damage).

Worked examples (every number is a value an author chooses and the owner reviews):

```
# A climb-out: 70 knots, 500 feet per minute, from 50 feet above the runway.
initial:
  quantities:
    altitude_ft: 1450
profile:
  flight_speed_kt: 70
  flight_vs_fpm: 500
  glide_speed_kt: 70
  glide_vs_fpm: -300
```

```
# A descent at 110 knots and 800 feet per minute from 6000 feet, banked 10 degrees.
initial:
  quantities:
    altitude_ft: 6000
profile:
  flight_speed_kt: 110
  flight_vs_fpm: -800
  flight_bank_deg: 10
```

```
# A situation with a reversed fuel temperature difference: the right tank is 4 degrees warmer.
profile:
  oat_c: -5
  fuel_temp_delta_c: -4
```

The check `npm run validate` adds one rule: L25, `profile.flight_nope` reads `unknown parameter "flight_nope"`, `profile.oil_cooling` reads `parameter "oil_cooling" is not a profile parameter; set it under parameters`, and a value outside the range reads like `2500 is outside -2000..2000 of parameter "flight_vs_fpm"`, each with file, line and column.
L25 also closes three holes (Phase 6 review, WR-03), each with file, line and column: (1) a profile parameter set under `parameters` is reported at `parameters.<id>` (`parameter "oat_c" is a profile parameter; set it under profile, where it holds from the first second`); a failure parameter stays allowed there, and `onset.parameters` may still change a profile parameter at the onset; (2) a key set in the profile and again in `parameters`, `onset.parameters` or the parameters of a variant is reported once per layer at `profile.<id>` (`the later layer silently wins from the onset on, so set it once`); (3) a start value of a quantity below its floor parameter (the aircraft data marks `terrain_ft` as the floor of `altitude_ft`, `floorOf`) is reported, for Easy and Medium and for Realistic separately, at the profile key, at `onset.parameters.<id>`, or at the start quantity when the floor is the normal value (`altitude_ft starts at 1000 in the Easy and Medium run, below terrain_ft 1400; the model would lift it ...`). A floor at or below the start value passes.

## Instructor review (phase 6, PFD)

Every number below is an instructor-review value, not an AFM figure; each is a data edit in `src/aircraft/da50rg/physics.ts` (constants and parameter normals), `quantities.ts` (ranges, bases and scales) or the scenario file named.

- **Glide defaults.** 90 kt and -700 fpm (`glide_speed_kt`, `glide_vs_fpm`); not the AFM best-glide figure.
- **Time constants.** Airspeed 5 s in flight (the ground regime follows within one step), vertical speed 3 s, pitch and bank 2 s (`AIRSPEED_TAU_MS`, `VERTICAL_SPEED_TAU_MS`, `ATTITUDE_TAU_MS`). The airspeed settles within 5 percent of its target after 15 s.
- **Fine scales.** `airspeed_kt`, `pitch_deg`, `bank_deg` and `altitude_ft` carry the scale 1e-4: a lag step near its target is below 0.005, and a climb of 500 fpm gains 0.8333 ft per step, which the default scale of 0.01 would round to 0.83 (2 ft a minute lost).
- **Pitch.** Target = flight-path angle (the scripted vertical speed divided by the airspeed floored at `PITCH_MIN_SPEED_KT` 40 times `PITCH_FPM_PER_KT_DEG` 1.7675) plus `PITCH_PER_KT` 0.06 degrees for every knot below the cruise speed 140. A level cruise reads 0, a 70 kt climb of 500 fpm about +8.2 degrees, a 90 kt glide of -700 fpm about -1.4 degrees. An aircraft that is not in the air (the ground regime, or at the terrain and not climbing) stands level at 0, so a parked aircraft does not read the nose-up angle of its speed before the onset.
- **Per scenario (altitude, speed).** ENG OIL TEMP 4500 ft; ALTN 1 FAIL and ALTN 2 FAIL 3500 ft; ELEC FIRE FLIGHT 4500 ft; ICING 7500 ft; ENGINE FIRE in flight 5500 ft; all level at 140 kt, ENGINE FIRE gliding at the defaults once the pilot stops the engine.
