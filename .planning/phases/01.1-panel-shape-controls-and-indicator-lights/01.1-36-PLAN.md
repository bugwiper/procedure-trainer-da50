## Owner decisions this plan builds (R3-Z2, 2026-10-21, all approved by the owner)

Problem: the owner often cannot pan because a finger or the mouse landed on a switch (for example while quickly zooming out), and a finger that lands on a switch should not toggle it. Decisions:
1. Latching controls (toggles, rockers, bats, circuit breakers, the flap levers' marker taps, FAN spots, the guard cover, the tap-toggle parking brake) act on RELEASE, not on press: a press is pending; if the pointer lifts within the slop (10 px touch, 5 px mouse) it is a tap and actuates; if it moves beyond the slop it is a pan (when zoomed) and the control discards the press; if a second pointer lands (pinch) the pending press is discarded and the pinch or pan proceeds, also over controls.
2. Momentary controls (ENGINE START, FADEC TEST, STALL/GEAR TEST, DE-ICE MAX and WIND SHIELD, LEVEL): the press commits after about 100 ms of being held still (pressed state and event), discarded on movement beyond the slop or a second pointer before that.
3. Drag controls (throttle, EMERGENCY GEAR EXTENSION, the gear and flaps lever handles, knob drags) keep their gesture; panning starts on the background or with two fingers.
4. Mouse: the same arbitration with the 5 px slop; a drag over a latching control pans, a click actuates.
5. Two-finger pinch and pan work everywhere, including over controls.
Tests: every test that expects actuation on press is updated; arbitration unit tests of a pure function; e2e for pan over a switch, pinch over a switch and tap (plans 37 to 39).

Planner decisions taken for gaps in the wording (recorded in the SUMMARY and in docs/PANEL-REVIEW.md by plan 41): a momentary press that lifts before the hold time without having moved or gained a second pointer is a quick tap and commits at the lift (press and release together), because lifting is not one of the discard causes and a mouse click is often shorter than 100 ms; pen counts as touch (10 px); a pointer of unknown type counts as touch; the background tap uses the same slop as a control press (so a mouse double click on the background now tolerates 5 px, not 10).

## Data tables

Table A1, the four classes of target (the gesture layer classifies a pointerdown by the element it lands on: no element carrying data-hit or data-detent is background; a registered press target gives its own class; an unregistered hit is own and owned from the first moment; the registry of targets arrives in plan 37, so in this plan every hit is own):

| Class | Targets | What a press is |
|-------|---------|-----------------|
| background | the panel background, display art, gaps between controls | a view pointer: a still, short, single press is a tap; beyond the slop it pans (when zoomed); two of them pinch |
| latch | toggles, rockers, bats, circuit breakers, guard covers, the tap-toggle flap, lever marker taps, FAN spots | pending until lift; lift within the slop is a tap; movement beyond the slop discards it and the pointer becomes a pan; a second pointer discards it and both pinch |
| momentary | push buttons and the spring-return toggle | pending; held still for MOMENTARY_HOLD_MS commits a press (held); lift before that without movement or a second pointer is a quick tap (press then release at the lift); movement beyond the slop or a second pointer before the commit discards it (pan or pinch follows); after the commit every move and every other pointer is ignored, and the lift or a cancel releases |
| own | lever handles, knob bodies (drag controls) and unregistered hits | the control keeps the pointer and the machine emits nothing for it; before the drag has travelled dragStartPx a second pointer takes it away (discard, then pinch); from that travel on the pointer is owned and every other pointer is ignored; a descriptor with dragStartPx 0 is owned from the start |

Table A2, constants (exported, the only source; tests derive from them):

| Constant | Value | Module |
|----------|-------|--------|
| TOUCH_SLOP_PX | 10 (touch, pen, unknown pointer type) | tap-intent.ts |
| MOUSE_SLOP_PX | 5 | tap-intent.ts |
| slopPx(kind) | the two values above by pointer kind | tap-intent.ts |
| TAP_MAX_MS | 300 (existing; a background tap and, from plan 40, a pair member must lift within it) | tap-intent.ts |
| MOMENTARY_HOLD_MS | 100 | press-arbiter.ts |
| DRAG_START_PX | 4 (the drag threshold the widgets use today as MIN_DRAG_PX) | press-arbiter.ts |

Table A3, the machine interface and effects (names are the contract the later plans use; payload fields beyond those listed are the executor's choice):

| Member | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| createPressArbiter() | returns the machine |
| down(sample, descriptor) | sample: pointer id, kind (mouse, touch, pen), primary flag, client x and y, time t; descriptor: class, key (the data-hit or data-detent value, null for the background) and dragStartPx for own; returns effects |
| move(sample), up(sample), cancel(pointerId), reset() | return effects; a pointer that is not tracked is ignored |
| tick(t), nextDeadline() | tick commits every pending momentary press whose deadline is at or before t and returns the effects; nextDeadline gives the earliest deadline or null (the glue schedules a timer from it) |
| stateOf(pointerId) | pending, held, view, owned, ignored or undefined, so the glue knows whether to capture the pointer |
| effect pending | latch press began or ended without a tap (on true or false), with id and key |
| effect tap | a tap: key (null for the background), the down point, the up point, the duration, and whether it was quick (within TAP_MAX_MS) |
| effect press, release | momentary commit (with the down point) and release; both are emitted together for a quick tap |
| effect discard | a pending latch, momentary or own press was taken away: the control clears its visual and does not act |
| effect pan | client-pixel deltas since the previous pan effect of the same pointer, the first one measured from the down point |
| effect pinch | the previous and the next midpoint and distance of the two view pointers |
| effect pair-break | something happened that ends a remembered tap pair: a momentary or own pointer went down, a pan or a pinch started, a pointer was cancelled or discarded |
