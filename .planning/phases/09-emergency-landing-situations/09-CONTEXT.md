<domain>
## Phase Boundary

A pilot who ends a situation with the flight conduct "Initiate emergency landing" can continue from the debrief into an emergency-landing situation: a simple view of the approach to an off-airport field (windscreen and PFD synthetic vision, the field growing with the descent), the pre-landing checklist of the booklet done on the panel (and ticked on the sheet where the panel has no control), graded like every situation. Two situations: without engine power and with engine power.

Out of scope: a landing model, a crash animation, a damage model, a field chosen by the user, several fields or terrain types, night and weather variants of the approach, the other printed landing checklists (gear up, defective tire, brakes: catalog todo), a precautionary-landing follow-on (the data model allows it, no scenario is planned), Realistic level for the landing situations (D-02; reversed by R-05, a landing starts at once on Realistic; a Realistic onset for a landing stays out).

</domain>

### Scope and order

### The follow-on (owner brief)
- **D-02:** The debrief offers the button "Continue with emergency landing" when the conduct selected at the end of the graded run is the emergency landing and the scenario declares a follow-on for it. The start is the normal start of a situation through the pack seam (the same path as Next situation): the panel starts at the baseline of the follow-on file, nothing is carried over from the previous run. The level is kept (the mode of the first brief was removed in Phase 7 R-01, so the level is the only thing to keep); the landing situations declare no onset, so a Realistic run shows the level card with Easy and Medium (planner decision, owner may veto: nothing begins on its own in a landing, the pilot has already decided; the existing rule "Realistic only where the file has an onset" applies unchanged). Each situation is graded on its own: the first run's result, recording and debrief are untouched by the offer and by what follows (a unit test compares the JSON of the result before and after). Retry in the follow-on repeats the follow-on; Back to panel leaves; no way back into the closed debrief. The button is offered after FAIL as well (the decision to land was made either way); it is not offered when the scenario names no follow-on. (The sentence about Realistic above is reversed by R-05; the single button by R-04; the condition that the conduct selected at the end is the emergency landing by R-13; the clause "nothing is carried over from the previous run" by R-14, superseded.)
- **D-03:** Data. Scenario file: the optional key `follow_on` inside `further`, a map from a conduct option id to `{ pack, scenario }` (format stays 1; today only `conduct_emergency` is used, the shape also fits a later precautionary follow-on). Aircraft data: the button text as `followOnLabel` of the conduct option (`further.ts`; the UI prints it, no text in `src/ui`). The old note "The emergency landing itself is not trained yet." (`debriefNote`) is shown only when no follow-on button is offered, so it stays true for a scenario without one. Lint L33 (per scenario): every key is a conduct option of the aircraft that the scenario lists in `accept` and the target ids match the id patterns. L34 (pack level, with L29 and L30 in `validate-all`): every target names a manifest entry of a checked pack and not the scenario itself. A target in a pack the access policy denies shows the existing load-failure card; the check stays out of the per-open lint (a paid pack needs no entry in the public data).
- **D-04 (planner reading; superseded by R-04 and R-07, kept for the record):** Which source leads where. ENGINE FIRE IN FLIGHT and T/O FIRE NO ABORT (their printed lists switch the engine off) lead to the situation without engine power; ELEC FIRE FLIGHT (the engine keeps running, the printed list switches only the electrics off) leads to the situation with engine power. No other shipped scenario accepts the emergency landing (D-13 of Phase 7). The engine-failure checklists of the catalog todo (booklet p11 and p12) are not shipped; when they are, they lead to the situation without engine power by the same key.

### The approach view (owner brief)
- **D-05:** A new scene id `outlanding` in aircraft data (`SCENE_IDS`, art in `src/ui/panel/windscreen/scenes.tsx`, synthetic vision style in `SyntheticVision.tsx`, both typed by the id tuple). The ground is the cruise landscape (sky, hills, field patches in the shared palette) with one landing field: a stubble-coloured quadrilateral with darker furrow stripes toward the vanishing point and a hedge edge, straight ahead and slightly right of the strut so the cowl does not hide it. The field is part of the scene, the user does not choose it, one fixed field for the scene (a second field would be a second scene id). Same flat-vector rules as the other scenes: no text, no filter, no mask, no animation, perspective arithmetic with + - * / only.
- **D-06:** The field grows with the descent through a cue, not through new simulation state: the cue `field_near` reads `altitude_ft`, level 0 at the terrain height plus 1200 ft (`FIELD_SIGHT_FT`, instructor-review value) and level 1 at the terrain height (a falling cue, the cue machinery already supports `from` above `to`). The terrain height is the aircraft's normal `terrain_ft` (1400), exported as one constant that physics and the cue table both read; a scenario on the scene keeps that terrain (a test over every scenario on the scene checks it; a different terrain would need its own cue). No new quantity, so there is no start-frame glitch and no second number to keep consistent. One pure function `fieldGeometry(level)` (new file `src/ui/panel/windscreen/field.ts`, + - * / and clamping, monotone: the near and far edges and the widths grow with the level; at level 1 the near edge lies below the picture) is read by the windscreen art and the synthetic vision, so they cannot disagree. The numbers are tuned after looking at screenshots (the visual check of Phase 7 D-18: scratch spec, screenshots to the session scratchpad, never committed).
- **D-07:** The PFD reads the same cue level from `bridge.cueLevels` and passes it to `SyntheticVision` (new optional prop `fieldLevel`); the free panel keeps the default scene, so nothing changes there. Preview flags show the scene without a scenario: `?scene=outlanding&q=altitude_ft:<value>`.

### Profile, physics and the end
- **D-08:** Physics: no landing model. With engine power the existing powered regime carries a descending profile (`flight_speed_kt`, a negative `flight_vs_fpm`). Without engine power the engine must be stopped from the first second without being a checklist row: one new profile parameter `engine_stopped` (0 or 1, normal 0, `profile: true`; in force from the first second at every level) makes `running()` false in `physics.ts`, so the existing glide regime (`glide_speed_kt`, `glide_vs_fpm`) and the stopped-propeller cue apply, and the electrical and CAS behaviour of a stopped engine follows from the existing models (the scenario file lists the messages that lint L10 and L26 report). The aircraft stands level on the terrain when the descent ends (the documented rule of Phase 6), values are instructor-review values listed in `docs/PANEL-REVIEW.md`. A scenario without the parameter is unchanged (a test compares an existing run before and after).
- **D-09:** The end. A new optional scenario section `finish` with a predicate `when` and own-words `text` (at most 12 words, like an outcome): the run completes by itself, exactly as if the pilot had pressed Procedure complete, in the first step in which the predicate holds (the approach reaches the field: `altitude_ft` at most the terrain height). It is graded like a pressed Procedure complete at that time: `grade` and its result are unchanged (a recording's `completedAt` is the finish time; the golden fixtures stay byte-identical). The pilot may press Procedure complete earlier. Outcomes stay FAIL-only; there is no touchdown outcome, no crash, no damage: a pilot who did not prepare fails through the missed critical steps, a prepared pilot passes. A named outcome fires first when both come in one step. The debrief prints the `finish` text as one line when the run ended that way (session flag, display state like the level, cleared with the run). Lint L32: `finish.when` resolves like an outcome predicate, does not hold at the start, and a do-nothing run of the file reaches it within the 600 s bound of L26, so a situation cannot hang until the 30-minute cap. The run end is the same code path as an outcome and the cap in `sim.ts`, `run.ts` and `session.ts`.

## Existing Code Insights

- The scenes are a typed tuple (`SCENE_IDS`) with a typed art record in `windscreen/scenes.tsx` and a typed style record in `SyntheticVision.tsx`; a new id fails the typecheck until both exist. Cue levels reach the windscreen as the `cues` prop of a scene and reach the PFD through `bridge.cueLevels`.

## Rework decisions (owner, 2026-11-03, after plans 09-01 to 09-03; plan 09-04)

Where a line says "planner", the owner may veto it.

- **R-01 Ditching is a situation of its own.** Booklet page 10, DITCHING (photo `.input/booklet-4.1-p10-ditching.jpg`, private): pack `landing` (free), scenario id `ditching`, manifest title DITCHING, catalog entry in the group NON-ANNUNCIATED EMERGENCY PROCEDURES with the printed tag DITCHING, placed by printed page directly after EMERGENCY LANDING (ENGINE OFF) (page 9). The text is typed verbatim: rows 1 to 10 with their actions, the two boxed rows of the note (Heavy swell with light wind: ditch parallel to the swell; Heavy wind: ditch into the wind), the boxed touchdown note, the box EVACUATE through doors and the box When outside inflate life vests (raft). A ditching is almost always without engine power (otherwise the pilot would reach land), so the situation starts like the engine-off landing (owner correction 2026-11-03): the profile sets `engine_stopped` 1, the existing glide regime applies, and the start is the dead-engine start of `landing-fixture-off` (09-02) and of the shipped `landing-engine-off` file: `rpm`, `load_pct`, `oil_pressure_bar`, `fuel_flow_gph` and `fuel_pressure_bar` 0 in `initial.quantities`, and the messages the lint reports under `annunciations`. The situation text may say the engine is out. Row 10 (Power ... 300FT/MIN rate of descent) is not a power setting here: the pilot must not try to flare completely as on land, because the height over water is hard to judge and a stall from too high is the danger; in the last part of the descent he reduces speed with a rate of descent of 300 ft/min and touches down at it, which is better than stalling and slapping in. Row 10 stays a plain printed line, typed verbatim and not graded.
- **R-02 What is graded (planner).** Panel rows where a control shows the desired state: row 7 Landing gear UP (`lv.gear`, critical), row 8 Flaps LDG (`lv.flaps`, standard). Every other row (mayday, heavy objects, backrests, keypad, sun visors, harnesses, speed, power) is a plain line the sheet lets the user tick; the notes and boxes are boxed comment rows. The gear starts down so that the printed row has an action, which makes a do-nothing run fail. The run ends at the water (`finish`) or at Procedure complete. The glide values are `glide_speed_kt` 77 (the printed Vref) and `glide_vs_fpm` -300 (the rate of row 10); the glide parameters are constants of the run and cannot vary with height, so minus 300 applies from the start to the water (an instructor-review value; a faster descent for the early part and 300 ft/min only near the water would need a height-dependent glide rate, which is not built). Severities and start values are instructor-review values for `docs/PANEL-REVIEW.md`.
- **R-03 The view.** A new scene id `ditching` (the cruise sky over a flat sea, no hills; wave bands that move toward the pilot and grow with the descent), one new cue `water_near` read from `altitude_ft` (falling, from `WATER_LEVEL_FT` plus `WATER_SIGHT_FT` to `WATER_LEVEL_FT`), one pure function `waterGeometry(level)` read by the windscreen and the PFD synthetic vision like `fieldGeometry`; no text, no filter, no mask, no animation, no magenta, element caps as the other scenes. The water is at sea level (`WATER_LEVEL_FT` 0, the profile sets `terrain_ft` to it); `ditching` joins `landingScenes` (conduct none).
- **R-04 The follow-on has several targets.** `follow_on.<conduct>` is a list of `{ pack, scenario, weight }` (weight an integer 0 to 100, the list one to four entries). On Easy and Medium the debrief shows one button per target, in the file's order, and the pilot chooses which to practise; the labels are aircraft data (a target table on the conduct option: Emergency landing, Ditching). On Realistic it shows one button with the generic label and the session draws the target by weight. Lint L33: key rules as before, plus no target twice and a weight sum above 0; L34: every target exists, none is the scenario itself. A weight 0 target is offered on Easy and Medium and never drawn (planner). Retry inside a follow-on repeats the same target and seed; Next situation keeps its rule.
- **R-05 A landing situation starts at once on Realistic (reverses D-02, planner for the machinery).** One pure predicate `offersRealistic(scenario)`: true when the scenario has an onset or its scene is one of the aircraft's landing scenes. It decides the level card, `setLevel` and the Next-situation start. A Realistic start without an onset starts the run at once from the initial block of the file: no briefing, nothing waits, no onset is drawn. It also applies when the pilot chooses a landing situation from the chooser (simplest: no flag for how a run was reached, nothing else to keep consistent). The Realistic debrief shows the reaction-time line only for a run that had an onset. The level card hint is unchanged. A Realistic onset for a landing stays deferred.
- **R-06 The draw.** `followOnFromSeed(seed, targets)`, pure integer arithmetic on `mulberry32` like the variant draw: the first value times the weight total, cumulative. The session draws the seed with its injected rng when the Realistic button is pressed and records `{ seed, from }` (the scenario id of the run whose debrief offered it) with the follow-on run (`Recording.followOn`); `grade` ignores it, so every golden recording is unchanged. Retry keeps it (`retrySeeds`); a replay of the draw from the recorded seed and the source file gives the recorded target.
