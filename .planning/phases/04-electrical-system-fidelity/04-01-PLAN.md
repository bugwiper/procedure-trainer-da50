## Decisions (assumptions taken in planning; the owner may veto any of them)

Sources: AFM Figure 1 "Electrical System" (private schematic `.input/elec-figure1-schematic.webp`), the AFM text of 07-33, 07-29, 03-06, 03A-02, 03A-03, 03A-11 and 04-05, the five wiring diagrams (400, 401, 402, 404, 406) in `.input/` (private; read and described in own words, never copied into the repo), and the owner's answers of 2026-10-23: a healthy alternator switched OFF raises its caution (E-07), diagram 401 shows the tie relay crossed out with ESS BUS on (E-01).

- **E-01** The bus tie relay follows the ESS BUS switch alone (Figure 1: its coil returns to ground through the ESS BUS switch and an ELECT. MASTER pole, and is fed by the 2 A master control line from the essential bus or by alternator 2's output through a diode). De-energised (ESS BUS off, the normal state) its main-to-essential contact is closed whatever the alternators do: main and essential bus are one bus through `cb.main_tie` and `cb.ess_tie`, and alternator 2 is cut off the main bus (diagrams 400, 404). Energised (ESS BUS on, ELECT. MASTER on) that contact opens (401, 402 and 406 cross it out), a bypass diode still passes main to essential (both tie breakers in; the essential bus never feeds the main bus through it), and a second contact joins alternator 2 to the main bus through the alternator 2 relay and `cb.alt2`. This replaces the first reading that the relay is closed only with alternator 1 online.
- **E-02** The main bus is powered by alternator 1 online; by alternator 2 online with the tie relay energised; or, with the tie contact closed, by the essential bus through it (both tie breakers in), that is by the battery when no alternator carries the essential bus. So with ESS BUS off the main bus draws on the battery, as the shipped ALTN 1 FAIL start has it and as AFM 04-05 has it (position and strobe lights, main bus breakers, are checked on the battery before engine start). With ESS BUS on the battery never feeds the main bus.
- **E-03** ELECT. MASTER has three poles in Figure 1: the battery relay coil (with the ground return of the tie relay coil), the alternator 2 relay and excitation relay coils (through the ALTERNATOR 2 switch) and the alternator 1 relay coil (through the ALTERNATOR 1 switch). Off: the battery bus and the essential bus lose the battery, both alternators drop out of every bus (as the shipped model), ESS BUS on does nothing, the hot bus keeps the battery and the charge does not change. Alternator 1 keeps its excitation battery and its direct line to ECU A and the main pump (E-04); no current is modelled for that.
- **E-04** Alternator fields: alternator 1 from the essential bus, its own output line or its excitation battery (relay closed by ALTERNATOR 1 on and ENGINE MASTER on); alternator 2 from its own output or its excitation battery (relay closed by ALTERNATOR 2 on and ELECT. MASTER on). The main battery charge never decides whether an alternator is online (excitation batteries assumed charged, not modelled). Feed lines of Figure 1 (diode pairs): ECU A and the main pump from the essential bus, the alternator 1 output line (ahead of its relay) or, with ALTERNATOR 1 on, its excitation battery; ECU B and the emergency pump from the main bus or the alternator 2 output line. So ECU A and the main pump stay powered with their breakers in while the essential bus is live or ALTERNATOR 1 is on (the "buffer protection" of 402 and 406), not whatever the bus state: ALTERNATOR 1 off with a dead essential bus leaves them unpowered. The AFM 03-06 warning that the engine stops once the battery is spent is not modelled (E-10).
- **E-05** Volts is the essential bus voltage. AFM 03A-11 and the CAS table define VOLTS LOW as the essential bus voltage below 25 V, the lower edge of the green Volts band; Figure 1 draws no G1000 voltage tap (only the current sensors and the ALT.2 FAIL relay go to the G1000; the regulators sense at the alternator output), so that the Volts bar is the same measurement was an inference, settled by the owner on 2026-10-23 (type-rating pages: the caution is defined on the essential bus). Value: the regulated base 28 V while an alternator carries the essential bus (through the closed tie contact or the bypass diode), the battery voltage (22 plus 4 times the charge fraction, as shipped) while the battery carries it, alone or together with the main bus, the scale minimum while it is dead. The battery rates stay (+10 and -25 per minute), so `main_bus_lost` still fires when the battery has run down (its id, text and predicate stay).
- **E-06** Currents, anchored on the base values (C-09). The additional loads (RACC) hang on alternator 2 alone and are shed by ESS BUS on, not by a failure: Figure 1 returns the ALT.2 FAIL relay coil to the ESS BUS line and the AFM note of 03A-02 says the same. Alternator 2 therefore carries 10 A whenever it is online with ESS BUS off, whatever alternator 1 does (alternator 1 failed, ESS BUS off reads 0 and 10 A, as shipped). Alternator 1 carries the 30 A main load alone with ESS BUS off; with ESS BUS on the 30 A are shared: 15 and 15 with both online (an instructor-review value, Figure 1 gives no sharing), 30 for one alone; each current is multiplied by its alternator's output parameter. The shipped 35 A for alternator 2 goes.
- **E-07** The control unit fault of alternator n is raised by: its output parameter below 1, its short parameter above 0, its breaker out, or its ALTERNATOR switch OFF. Not by the engine state or ELECT. MASTER. It is a hidden quantity (`alt1_fault`, `alt2_fault`, 0 or 1, follows in one step) because CAS rules read quantities; the rule reads a band, so no number enters the code. A scenario whose failure exists from the start sets it in `initial.quantities` (Easy, Medium); on Realistic the model raises it at the onset.
- **E-08** Alternator 2 gets the parameters `alt2_output` and `alt2_short` and the trip `alt2_breaker` (cb.alt2 out again 2 s after it is pushed in with a short), as alternator 1.
- **E-09** Breakers in the model are those in a feed line of Figure 1: alt1, alt2, batt, ess_tie, main_tie, master_ctrl (2 A: feeds the tie relay coil and the avionic master relay coil from the essential bus), ecu_a, ecu_b, main_pump, emerg_pump, av_bus. A pulled breaker removes its own feed only. Figure 1 puts `cb.alt1` and `cb.alt2` on the main-bus side only and does not show how the control unit would see a popped one; the model keeps the shipped rule that a popped breaker takes its alternator off every bus (0 A, caution), as the printed circuit-breaker row and the variants have it. The panel art groups some breakers differently (EMERG PUMP in the essential outline); that is panel layout (G-0) and stays, the model follows Figure 1.
- **E-10** Consumers that change a quantity now: the EMERG FUEL PUMP (fuel pressure, Engine Fire feed) and RACC (alternator 2 current). ECU A, ECU B, main pump and avionics are derived and tested but change nothing: the engine runs on any bus state. They also need ENGINE MASTER on (a four-pole switch in Figure 1; the emergency pump has its own switch), and the avionic bus needs AV MASTER on, `cb.av_bus` and a live master control line.
- **E-11** The emergency pump hangs on the main bus (or the alternator 2 output line): it has power with its switch on, `cb.emerg_pump` in and a live main bus. With the engine stopped the main bus is live while ELECT. MASTER is on and ESS BUS off (battery through the closed tie, E-02), so the pump still runs and feeds the fire there (`FIRE_FEED_PUMP_ONLY` and its 0.6 stay). It has no power, no pressure and no feed with ELECT. MASTER off, with ESS BUS on and no alternator, with its breaker out or with an empty battery. This differs from the first answer 3 of 2026-10-23 (no feed once the engine has stopped) in the state ELECT. MASTER on, ESS BUS off, which Figure 1 and AFM 04-05 force; the owner confirmed the pump runs there. Fallback if declined: pump power also requires an alternator online, a one-line change in `electricalState`. Golden verdicts are unaffected (task 3 proves it).
