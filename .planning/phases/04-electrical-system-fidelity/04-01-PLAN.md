## Decisions (assumptions taken in planning; the owner may veto any of them)

Sources: the five wiring diagrams and the AFM power generation text in `.input/` (private; read, never copied into the repo).

- **E-01** The bus tie relay is closed only with alternator 1 online and ESS BUS off. It is open otherwise: diagram 401 shows it open with ESS BUS on although both alternators work, 402 and 406 open. When it is open the diagrams still show a one-way path from the main bus to the essential bus and the battery (a main bus carried by alternator 2 charges the battery); the battery never feeds the main bus. Both paths need `cb.ess_tie` and `cb.main_tie` in.
- **E-02** The main bus is powered by alternator 1 online, or by alternator 2 online with ESS BUS on. Never by the battery.
- **E-03** ELECT. MASTER off opens the alternator relays and the battery bus (as the shipped model: both alternators drop out). The hot bus keeps the battery; the battery charge does not change.
- **E-04** An alternator field is energised from the battery bus or, failing that, from its own excitation battery (assumed charged, not modelled), so the battery charge never decides whether an alternator is online. The same buffer protection keeps ECU A and the main pump powered, whatever the bus state, while their breakers are in.
- **E-05** Volts is the essential bus voltage (the engine instruments sit on the essential bus): the regulated base 28 V while the main bus carries it, the battery voltage (22 plus 4 times the charge fraction, as shipped) while only the battery does, the scale minimum while it is dead. The battery rates stay (+10 and -25 per minute), so `main_bus_lost` still fires when the battery that carries the essential bus has run down (its id, text and predicate stay).
- **E-06** Currents, anchored on the base values (C-09): alternator 1 carries the 30 A main load alone and alternator 2 the 10 A additional loads (RACC); with ESS BUS on and both online each carries half (15 A); one alone carries all 30 A; RACC runs only with both online and ESS BUS off (every failure sheds it); each current is multiplied by its alternator's output parameter. The shipped 35 A for alternator 2 goes.
- **E-07** The control unit fault of alternator n is raised by: its output parameter below 1, its short parameter above 0, its breaker out, or its ALTERNATOR switch OFF. Not by the engine state or ELECT. MASTER. It is a hidden quantity (`alt1_fault`, `alt2_fault`, 0 or 1, follows in one step) because CAS rules read quantities; the rule reads a band, so no number enters the code. A scenario whose failure exists from the start sets it in `initial.quantities` (Easy, Medium); on Realistic the model raises it at the onset.
- **E-08** Alternator 2 gets the parameters `alt2_output` and `alt2_short` and the trip `alt2_breaker` (cb.alt2 out again 2 s after it is pushed in with a short), as alternator 1.
- **E-09** Breakers in the model are those in a feed line of the diagrams: alt1, alt2, batt, ess_tie, main_tie, ecu_a, ecu_b, main_pump, emerg_pump, av_bus. A pulled breaker removes its own feed only. The panel art groups some of them differently (EMERG PUMP in the essential outline); that is panel layout (G-0) and stays, the model follows the diagrams.
- **E-10** Consumers that change a quantity now: the EMERG FUEL PUMP (fuel pressure, Engine Fire feed) and RACC (alternator 2 current). ECU A, ECU B, main pump and avionics are derived and tested but change nothing: the engine runs on any bus state.
- **E-11** The pump is on the main bus, which needs an alternator, so with the engine stopped or ELECT. MASTER off it has no power. The Engine Fire feed "pump alone" (0.6) can no longer occur and goes. Golden verdicts are unaffected (task 3 proves it).
